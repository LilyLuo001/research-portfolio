## What would change the ranking

Candidate 2 falls below Candidate 3 if portfolio continuity fails for a majority of exact conversions or matched controls show persistent differential pretrends. Candidate 3 becomes a standalone headline if at least six historical activation dates have complete outcomes and leave-one-date-out conclusions are stable. Candidate 4 rises after at least one year of post-launch data and wider sponsor adoption. Candidate 6 cannot rise on novelty alone; it needs an observed creation/redemption first stage and enough independent FOMC dates.

The ranking deliberately does not reward the number of stock rows. It rewards independent shocks, observable treatment intensity, continuity, and a defendable assignment mechanism.

## Ranking of the four proposed headline questions

1. **Candidate 2 — asset-management behavior after the same strategy acquires an ETF channel.** Strongest. It aligns the fully treated fund-level unit with observable monthly outcomes and allows exact-portfolio validation.
2. **Candidate 3 — an exchange-traded claim on an unchanged portfolio.** Cleanest experiment, but currently too few independent activation dates to carry the entire paper alone.
3. **Candidate 1 — ETF architecture and information transmission into asset prices.** Economically important, but the current P1 stock dose and Refraction first-stage/data deficits make the claim premature.
4. **Candidate 4 — unified omnibus.** Reject in its listed form. A disciplined architecture paper may combine conversions and share classes, but earnings, FOMC, TAQ, every fund outcome, and every market-quality outcome cannot all be coequal headlines.
