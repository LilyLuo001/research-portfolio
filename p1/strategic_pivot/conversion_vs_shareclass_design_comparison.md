## What each design can claim

The conversion design targets the consequences of replacing the mutual-fund wrapper with an ETF wrapper. It could identify a conditional effect only under defensible parallel trends, no anticipation, no spillovers, continuity, and common support. It does **not** mechanically hold strategy, shareholders, fees, disclosure, and portfolio composition fixed. Those are treatment components or possible confounds that must be measured.

If institutional verification establishes a pro-rata claim on the **same underlying
portfolio**, the share-class design targets the consequence of adding exchange
trading, creation/redemption, and ETF distribution while conventional mutual
shares remain. Conditional identification still requires no
anticipation/spillovers and a defensible counterfactual. It could offer cleaner
portfolio continuity, but it does not identify a full wrapper replacement or
eliminate endogenous launch selection.

The designs therefore should not be pooled into a single treatment coefficient. A publishable triangulation would require the novelty and F0 gates to establish a feasible fund-level conversion design, with institutionally verified same-portfolio share-class activations as separate mechanism/external-validity evidence and stock-level dose results only as secondary transmission evidence.

## Historical decision rule — superseded

No design is currently promoted. Any future headline eligibility must be frozen
from pre-assignment identity and announced-change information, matched-control
support, and pretrends. Realized portfolio continuity is reported only as a
post-treatment diagnostic. The 19 historical Vanguard records cannot be called
usable until their institutional relationship and timing are verified. Any new
threshold or counterfactual requires a separate specification and pilot before
construction.
