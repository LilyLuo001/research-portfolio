# P1 literature evidence handoff — 2026-09-13

**Scope/status.** I read the existing `p1/lit` matrices, then re-verified the named sources at author, publisher, Fed, or journal versions. This is evidence for contribution/design adjudication, **not a novelty verdict**. Targeted outlet coverage (AER, QJE, JPE, Econometrica, REStud, JF, JFE, RFS, JFQA, *Management Science*) surfaced the directly relevant publications below in JFE/RFS/MS and the closest accounting papers in JAE/JAR; it is not evidence that no other paper exists.

* **Saglam–Tuzun (2025), “Implications of Growth in ETFs: Evidence from Mutual Fund to ETF Conversions,” FEDS Notes, 19 Nov. 2025, DOI 10.17016/2380-7172.3909.** Direct same-setting predecessor, now an official note (not merely the earlier owner-supplied citation). Treatment is the June 11, 2021 Dimensional conversion and stock-level change in ETF ownership; outcomes are March-to-September changes in daily-return volatility and TAQ effective spreads. Cross-sectional OLS controls for market-cap and volume changes, rather than an earnings-event DiD (Fed §§2–3.2, esp. lines 20–26, 36–45, 58–61, 84–107). It reports lower volatility and spreads with greater transferred ETF ownership. Remaining P1 increment, conditional on design validity: exposure-gradient change in *underlying stocks’ earnings-response timing* rather than six-month market quality; the note itself does not establish that increment or exogeneity.
* **Glosten–Nallareddy–Zou (2021), “ETF Activity and Informational Efficiency of Underlying Securities,” *Management Science* 67(1):22–47, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2019.3427; verified accessible Sept. 2019 SSRN revision.** Treatment is ETF activity/ownership, outcome short-run efficiency (timely systematic-earnings incorporation, co-movement, PEAD, share lending); the accessible abstract establishes associations, not a conversion design (SSRN abstract, pp. 1–2). Result: stronger improvement in weak-information environments, driven by systematic earnings information. Exact P1 increment remains a conversion-package exposure gradient and a preregistered timing-shape contrast around own announcements; it must not claim a novel earnings-efficiency outcome absent a sharper comparison.
* **Huang–O’Hara–Zhong (2021), “Innovation and Informed Trading: Evidence from Industry ETFs,” *RFS* 34(3):1280–1316, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhaa077.** Treatment: industry-ETF inception/membership and industry-risk exposure; outcomes: hedge-fund pre-announcement positions, ETF short interest, and PEAD. Identification includes DiD at inception; result is lower PEAD especially for high-industry-risk stocks, consistent with hedging-enabled informed trading (publisher abstract; original Feb. 2018 MS pp. 1–2). P1 must distinguish new industry baskets from conversion of an existing fund and cannot equate its event curve with a new outcome.
* **Sammon (2024 online; 2025 issue), “Passive Ownership and Price Informativeness,” *Management Science* 71(6):4582–4598, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2023.00836.** Several designs, including quasi-exogenous passive-ownership increases from index additions/rebalancing (author draft, Oct. 2023, pp. 1–2); outcome is information anticipated in price before earnings. Published abstract estimates a decline of about one-quarter of its sample mean (one-sixth SD) from the long-run rise in passive ownership (publisher abstract, lines 210–15). P1’s exact increment is wrapper conversion, not passive ownership broadly; direction must remain two-sided because conversion may alter clientele/taxes as well as trading.
* **Moussawi–Shen–Velthuis (2025), “The Role of Taxes in the Rise of ETFs,” *RFS* 38(10):2988–3039, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhaf044.** Tax burden variation identifies tax efficiency as a driver of high-net-worth migration from mutual funds to ETFs; outcome is after-tax returns/capital migration, not price discovery (publisher/IDEAS abstract). It estimates 1.05% annual after-tax ETF advantage. Exact P1 relevance is an identification constraint: conversions can be selected for tax/clientele reasons; not a competing earnings outcome.
* **Box–Davis–Evans–Lynch (2021), “Intraday Arbitrage between ETFs and their Underlying Portfolios,” *JFE* 141:1078–95, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.04.023.** 423 passive U.S. equity ETFs, 2006–15; minute returns/order imbalances, PVAR/IRFs and FEVD (pp. 1079–80). ETF returns/order flow do not lead constituents; quote adjustments resolve discrepancies (abstract; conclusion pp. 1093–94). P1 therefore cannot label a wrapper coefficient as AP-arbitrage isolation; remaining increment is the conversion package’s earnings timing, if estimable.
* **Grégoire–Martineau (2022), “How Is Earnings News Transmitted to Stock Prices?” *JAR* 60(1):261–97, DOI 10.1111/1475-679X.12394.** After-hours earnings announcements; quotes versus trades are the outcome/measurement comparison. They find quote changes—not trades—largely incorporate surprises, with asymmetric bid/ask adjustment (author version, Nov. 2019, pp. 1–2; published abstract). P1’s increment is treatment heterogeneity, but this paper makes live-quote availability and release-time accuracy prerequisites, not optional robustness.
* **Christensen–Timmermann–Veliyev (2026 working paper), “Warp Speed Price Moves: Jumps after Earnings Announcements,” arXiv:2601.08962, 13 Jan. 2026.** Uses a unique after-hours dataset and a microstructure-noise-robust jump test; finds earnings almost always induce jumps and post-2016 strategy returns consistent with efficient formation (abstract). No ETF treatment. It narrows P1’s measurement claim: a price-timing design needs its own valid jump/quote framework, not daily proxies.
* **Closest additional earnings × ETF paper: Bhojraj–Mohanram–Zhang (2020), “ETFs and Information Transfer Across Firms,” *Journal of Accounting and Economics* 70(2–3):101336, DOI 10.1016/j.jacceco.2020.101336.** ETF ownership/type (sector versus broad), earnings response/PEAD and leader-to-follower information transfer; firm-level association plus four-quarters-before/after first ETF ownership quasi-experiment (author manuscript §§3–5). Sector ownership is associated with higher ERCs, less PEAD and stronger follower reactions; broad ETFs differ. This is materially closer than the existing matrix recognized: P1 must separate *own-firm* announcement timing from cross-firm transfer and conversion from first ETF membership; no claim that idiosyncratic earnings responsiveness is new until that separation is implemented.

**Access limits.** Publisher full text was inaccessible for Moussawi et al.; its author 2022 draft was accessible. Precise specifications/locators for GNZ’s final version and Sammon’s individual designs remain unverified beyond accessible abstract/draft. No inference of absence/novelty follows from these limits.
