## Independent mathematics and numerical reproduction

For a horizon, write `y=X0γ+Zβ+ε`, weights `W`, `Q=√W`, and `R=M_(QX0)QZ`. At full relevant rank, `B=(R'R)^−1 R'Q`, so `βhat=By`. Across horizons and stacks, `V_hk=B_h Ω_hk B_k'`. Any explicit coefficient/horizon contrast has variance `L V L'`. This matches the contract and production `weighted_fwl` lines 182–207. Scaling every treatment column by 1000 scales its coefficient and influence by 1/1000 and variance by 1/10^6; converting the reporting unit back leaves the estimand and its precision unchanged.

If each wave is residualized with its own nuisance terms and `A_w=R_w'R_w`, a pooled scalar coefficient equals `Σ A_w βhat_w / Σ A_w`. Equal total row weight in a wave does not force equal `A_w`. The multivariate equivalent has matrix weights, which can mix coefficient directions. Equal-wave effects require explicit separate-wave target influences averaged over a fixed identified wave set, with cross-wave covariance. Executed counterexample: two equally weighted two-row waves, residual doses `(-1,1)` and `(-3,3)`, effects 0 and 1; pooled WLS returns **0.9**, whereas the declared equal-wave target is **0.5**.

Let the transported target-population counterfactual slope curve be `b0`, `f_h=b0_h/b0_T`. An amplitude-only treatment curve `β=a f` satisfies `q_h=β_h−f_hβ_T=0`. Thus rejecting this proportional restriction can distinguish a departure in shape; claiming pure redistribution additionally needs a directional pattern and equivalence of the terminal effect within a signed margin. An early significant coefficient or `β_h−β_T` alone fails the amplitude-only counterexample. If `f` is estimated, its uncertainty and covariance with treatment coefficients must enter inference; weak/sign-uncertain `b0_T` blocks the normalized interpretation. Different baseline shapes across dose groups require a stated common target or group-specific amplitude reference. The contract correctly leaves these assumptions and margins open.
