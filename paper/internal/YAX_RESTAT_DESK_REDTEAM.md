# ReStat desk-rejection red team

## Strongest skeptical case

The manuscript could be read as a large robustness appendix to a young-worker employment result that it did not discover. It compares several exposure indices, confirms that definitions differ, and reruns a familiar outcome design. The absence of observed adoption and causal variation prevents the paper from deciding which exposure measure captures economically relevant AI use. A negative coefficient under multiple measures may simply reflect their shared correlation with white-collar computerization. The extensive taxonomy, support, and influence audits may demonstrate careful practice without generating an independent economic finding. The mobility exercise documents disagreement but then shows that broad assortative matching explains nearly all unusual excess conflict. An editor could therefore conclude that the paper offers a useful replication protocol rather than a general contribution suited to ReStat.

## Response (150 words)

The paper's contribution is not another coefficient check. It identifies the treatment object consumed by each economic statement and shows that robustness cannot be inherited across those objects. A tail comparison depends on weighted group membership; a continuous model depends on residual score variation; a mobility claim depends on pairwise ranking. Six defensible architectures yield a stable aggregate tail direction while producing sharply different rankings, conditional information, and comparisons. That coexistence is the finding. Taxonomy repair further shows that support is part of treatment construction, not a clerical afterthought: it changes which occupations enter the estimand. The mobility benchmark then bounds interpretation rather than nullifying it, distinguishing frequent architectural disagreement from unusually conflict-heavy realized pairings. Together these results provide a reusable framework for any setting in which researchers merge a generated index into economic data. The paper therefore advances measurement architecture and statement-specific interchangeability, using early-career employment as its laboratory.
