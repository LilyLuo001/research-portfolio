# Expected referee questions

## Why no causal AI-adoption identification?

The object is occupational potential exposure, not observed adoption. The paper asks which statements survive defensible treatment constructions while holding the outcome design fixed; it labels the employment estimates as associations.

## Why is potential exposure economically relevant without observed adoption?

Potential exposure is the treatment used in a growing empirical literature and can structure expectations, investment, and task susceptibility. Its relevance does not license an adoption claim; the paper evaluates what this commonly used object can support.

## Why not run leave-one-measure outcome regressions after the family-disagreement result?

That search was not part of the frozen design and would make outcome-dependent measure selection especially tempting. Treatment-side stability and fixed-treatment occupation deletion answer bounded diagnostic questions without adding a specification grid.

## Why are individual family-centroid intervals normal rather than wild-score?

The A-minus-E contrast exactly inherits the stored wild-score distribution for the disagreement coordinate. Individual centroid intervals require a different linear combination; draw-level F/G shifts were not retained, so they use the serialized covariance matrix with a normal critical value.

## Why report Q5 versus Q1 with non-monotone intermediate quintiles?

The predeclared estimand is an extreme-tail contrast. The paper displays the full quintile profile and explicitly rejects a monotone dose-response interpretation.

## Is the headline driven by software developers?

No. Software developers rank tenth in absolute fixed-treatment deletion influence, and deleting them makes the coefficient more negative: from -0.131074 to -0.138787.

## Why is fast food influential?

Fast food and counter workers are a heavily weighted Q1 occupation with favorable young-relative movement. Deleting it moves the contrast toward zero. This explains its statistical influence, not the economic cause of its movement.

## Does that imply a pandemic-recovery mechanism?

No. It reinforces the need to interpret the coefficient alongside post-pandemic normalization, but the occupation-deletion audit cannot distinguish restaurant recovery, wages, labor supply, or other mechanisms.

## Why did prospective power overstate realized precision?

The headline and paired standard errors were understated by factors of about 3.65 and 3.17. A code audit rules out a simple temporal-independence error and does not support excessive nominal-information diffusion as the explanation. No unique cause is claimed.

## Why use CPS rather than payroll or administrative data?

CPS supplies nationally comparable age groups, occupation codes, and longitudinal links across employers. Administrative studies offer complementary precision and adoption detail; they do not remove uncertainty in the exposure treatment merged to those outcomes.

## Why does common support not make treatments identical?

Common support fixes the set of occupations, not scores, quintile memberships, residual variation, or rankings. The downstream treatment remains architecture-specific.

## Why is mobility disagreement meaningful if the hard benchmark removes unusual excess conflict?

The 53.28 percent conflict rate establishes that architectures often label the same realized move differently. The benchmark sharpens, rather than erases, that claim: observed worker pairing is not unusually conflict-heavy beyond broad assortativity.
