\section*{Executive response}

The title is now ``Constructed Exposure Measures and Statement-Specific Robustness: Evidence from Early-Career Employment.'' The abstract no longer organizes the paper around a causal or generally robust employment effect. Four findings drive the revision: outside architectures attenuate the employment contrast; Q1 is a consequential reference; the age and post-period comparisons are heterogeneous; and mobility disagreement falls materially with movement size while aggregate direction remains stable.

\section*{Point-by-point responses}

\textbf{1. R1-M1; R2-3.4 and 3.10: architecture space and primitives.}

\emph{Concern.} Six scores were presented too readily as six independent measurements of one object, and task-family algebra was underexplained.

\emph{Action and finding.} The paper now defines two selected families with six dependent implementations and distinguishes broad AI capability, direct LLM acceleration, and software-complemented exposure. On 444 occupations, raw beta equals $(\alpha+\gamma)/2$ to $7.0\times10^{-13}$. Separate standardization breaks the midpoint identity but adds no primitive. A fixed-support $D+\lambda S$ grid is reported under fixed and restandardized scales. We never jointly enter alpha, beta, and broad as primitive regressors.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} The contribution is construction sensitivity, not latent-truth recovery. Main Section 2; Online Appendix A and I.

\textbf{2. R1-M1 and concluding assessment: omitted architectures, Webb, OECD, and literature scope.}

\emph{Concern.} The six-measure set could be selected and Webb software might be confused with Webb AI.

\emph{Action and finding.} An outcome-independent admission rule accepted Webb AI and the reversed OECD capability gap. Webb AI estimates $-0.0649$ with interval $[-0.1439,0.0141]$; OECD estimates $-0.0110$ with interval $[-0.0864,0.0645]$. Webb software remains a computerization control and correlates .7021 with Webb AI. Frey--Osborne is retained as automation risk, not relabeled AI. A declared top-ten-journal search and primary-source ledger were completed.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} The all-negative conclusion is bounded to two selected families. Main Sections 1, 2, and 5; Appendix A/E; literature ledger.

\textbf{3. R2-3.1: generic occupational placebos and synthetic architectures.}

\emph{Concern.} A generic digital, educated, or cognitive occupation gradient could mimic the AI pattern.

\emph{Action and finding.} On identical 363-occupation support, beta is $-0.1142$; wage, education, cognition, telework, and STEM are $-0.0185,+0.0093,+0.0270,-0.0240,-0.0161$. Paired beta-minus intervals exclude zero for education, cognition, and telework, not wage or STEM. Telework/STEM use natural groups because quintiles collapse. In 999 within-SOC2 permutations, the observed lower-tail probability is .219 (centered two-sided .434).

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Beta is more negative than several selected benchmarks, but the permutation does not establish AI specificity or observational-CI validity. Main Section 5 and Figure 2; Appendix E.

\textbf{4. R1-M2; R2-3.2: Q1 dominance, paths, and exclusions.}

\emph{Concern.} The original Q5--Q1 result could be driven by favorable Q1 evolution rather than uniquely adverse Q5 outcomes.

\emph{Action and finding.} Q5--Q2 is $-0.0456$ and Q5--Q4 $-0.0340$, with both intervals spanning zero; Q5--Q3 is $-0.0833$ with an interval excluding zero. Separate young stock, older stock, and ratio paths are shown with 2019 and 2022 normalization. SOC35, SOC15, SOC43, and approximate IND1990 leisure/hospitality exclusions remain negative. Fast-food deletion attenuates the coefficient to $-0.1106$. Stable/reclassified tails and estimator information are reported.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} The paper now says the low-exposure reference contributes materially and does not infer a monotone dose response or causal occupation contribution. Main Section 5 and Figure 3; Appendix E/J.

\textbf{5. R1-M2/event study; R2-3.3 and 3.9: age composition and time heterogeneity.}

\emph{Concern.} Pooling ages 26--65 and averaging the post period might manufacture the headline.

\emph{Action and finding.} Estimates for 22--25 versus 26--35, 26--45, 36--55, and 51--65 are $-0.1307,-0.1475,-0.1464,-0.0978$. The nearest-group result is virtually identical to the pooled $-0.1311$; only the 51--65 paired difference is detected. The six-bin model gives +.0645 for ages 31--40 relative to 51--65. Ages 18--21 versus 51--65 give $-0.1050$ with an interval spanning zero. Era estimates are $-0.1356,-0.0783,-0.1629$ for 2023, 2024, and 2025--26; the last differs from 2024, not 2023.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Comparison age matters, but a prime-age pooling story does not explain the result relative to ages 26--35. The post period is nonmonotone. Main Section 5, Figures 4--5; Appendix E/F.

\textbf{6. R1-M3; R2-3.9: calendar, reconstruction, samples, and weights.}

\emph{Concern.} Missing March and October samples were conflated; sample counts and bridge mechanics were unclear.

\emph{Action and finding.} The original extract requested March ASEC `03s`; five basic `03b` samples were restored. October 2025 was not collected during the shutdown. The 108-month coefficient $-0.1311$ becomes $-0.1346$ over 113 months. The bridge uses 56 one-to-many source codes representing 20.03 percent of early stock and mechanically preserves source age ratios across components. Post-2020 and stable Census-2010 sensitivities are reported. A unified table reconciles 463/468/465/444 occupations, cells, respondent equivalents, stocks, and age-period coverage.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Calendar omission does not explain the estimate; bridge-based reconstruction is transparent and bounded by alternative estimands. Main Section 3; Appendix B; calendar memo.

\textbf{7. R1-M3/4: estimator, sparse cells, and seasonality.}

\emph{Concern.} Weighted grouped-binomial estimation and cell support needed a derivation and diagnostics.

\emph{Action and finding.} The binary conditional-Poisson equivalence is derived for the actual two-age implementation. Weighted stocks are pseudo-likelihood inputs, not survey-weighted independent trials. Primary support has 13,305 zero-young, 1,036 zero-older, and 965 empty occupation-month cells. Minimum 100/250/500 respondent-equivalent thresholds leave coefficients between $-0.1311$ and $-0.1326$; the unweighted respondent-equivalent construction gives $-0.1308$.

The declared occupation-age seasonality model did not converge with 5,148 extra nuisance parameters. We report that failure rather than substitute a more favorable model.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Main Sections 3--4; Appendix B/D/E; unresolved-items register.

\textbf{8. R1-M4/5; R2-3.5: joint inference, percentages, information, and precision.}

\emph{Concern.} The IUT and simultaneous band were conflated, effective counts overinterpreted, and planning precision unexplained.

\emph{Action and finding.} One-sided marginal tail areas are .045/.005/.006/.008/.001/.001; the IUT maximum is .045. The predeclared simultaneous critical value is 2.26036 and limiting upper bound +.01858, so that criterion still fails. Percentage intervals use the monotone $100(e^\beta-1)$ transformation and are relative-stock ratios. We distinguish 468 nominal clusters, 53.26 effective residual support, 43.30 effective fitted information, and .0205 maximum LOCO movement. Two-way occupation/month SE is .0449 versus .0444 one-way.

The primary and paired realized/prospective ratios are 3.649 and 3.167 on identical targets. Existing simulations do not uniquely allocate the miss. A literal 199-draw residual-wild full refit produced zero admissible grouped-binomial pseudo-outcomes; no clipping was used.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Main Section 4; Appendix D/K.

\textbf{9. R1-M7; R2-3.6: family outcome refits and A/E algebra.}

\emph{Concern.} Treatment-side correlations could not establish outcome-coefficient robustness, especially without alpha.

\emph{Action and finding.} The outcome model was refit excluding each measure. No-alpha estimates are $F=-0.0330$ and $G=+0.0342$, both intervals excluding zero. G intervals cross zero when beta or broad is omitted, but not when an AIOE measure is omitted. A representative AIOE/beta model and direct D/S model are reported. Draw-consistent A/E transformation gives $\gamma_A=.0248$ and $\gamma_E=-.0707$ in original family units.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Alpha alone does not generate the asymmetry, but task-family construction affects its precision. The A/E display is the same model, not corroboration. Main Section 6; Appendix I.

\textbf{10. R1-M6; R2-3.7: mobility thresholds, persistence, and aggregate conclusions.}

\emph{Concern.} Zero-threshold sign conflict could exaggerate economically tiny movements.

\emph{Action and finding.} Both material-move and substantial-opposition definitions were implemented at 0/.1/.25/.5 SD and percentile-rank thresholds. Zero-threshold conflict is 53.28 percent; movement-mass weighting gives 40.10 percent; substantial opposition at .5 SD is 14.00 percent. Reversal and persistent samples are similar. No-alpha and within-family results, denominators, named pairs, and support are reported. Aggregate moving-higher shares remain 49.2--50.5 percent.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} The revised paper emphasizes scale-sensitive ranking fragility with stable aggregate direction. Main Section 7; Appendix H.

\textbf{11. R1-M6; R2-3.7: hard-rematching benchmark.}

\emph{Concern.} The prior 0.96-point excess was near a threshold; support and generated self-transitions needed auditing.

\emph{Action and finding.} The 30,170 strata, 200,000 pseudo-units, 999 draws, and 98.31-percent coverage were verified. The revised benchmark enforces no self-transition and preserves margins. Realized .5328 versus benchmark .5232 leaves .0096. Monte Carlo SE of the mean is .000017. Pseudo-size checks give .0092 and .0091 gaps. The exact unconstrained expectation .5029 permits 3.66-percent self-transitions and is not applied to the constrained null.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} The result is near-threshold and benchmark-dependent, not equivalence. Main Section 7; Appendix H.

\textbf{12. R1 contribution; R2-3.8: taxonomy contribution.}

\emph{Action and finding.} Computer/math coverage rises 3.33 to 97.7 percent. The four-row sequence is $-.01885\to-.01920\to-.03156\to-.02940$. Stable coarser taxonomy and post-2020 results are added.

\emph{Interpretation/location.} Repair is a required measurement operation; its substantive effect is mainly estimand composition. Main Section 3; Appendix B.

\section*{Principled non-adoptions and remaining blockers}

We do not call the within-SOC2 exercise randomization inference because exchangeability is unsupported. We do not apply the unconstrained product-marginal expectation to a no-self benchmark. We do not claim equivalence from intervals containing zero. We do not infer coding error from immediate reversals. We do not invent a survey-design correction, a numerical SESOI, a latent true exposure, or a decomposition of the ADP--Denmark disagreement.

The only document-input blocker is the absence of the full referee files. Empirically, the seasonality and literal full-refit wild diagnostics failed for stated computational/statistical reasons. These items and the exact provisional wording are preserved in the unresolved-items register.
