\section{Broad-Family Support, Profiles, and Influence}\label{app:families}

\subsection{Family-by-quintile support}

Table~\ref{tab:app_family_support} reports, for every SOC two-digit family, the number of retained occupations, preperiod stock share, exposure range, quintiles present, direct-tail overlap, and nuisance-adjusted Q5 information share.  Only arts/media (SOC27), health practitioners (SOC29), health support (SOC31), and sales (SOC41) span Q1 and Q5 under the reconstructed comparison.  Other families connect the Q5--Q1 regression coefficient through intermediate quintiles and the imposed common Q2--Q5 slopes.

Table~\ref{tab:app_family_information} makes the loss and concentration of identifying information explicit.  Family-by-month conditioning retains 29.7 percent of the pooled Q5 target's nuisance-adjusted information, while the direct-tail comparison has only 6.2 effective occupations and its five largest occupation contributions supply 77.8 percent of information.  Its normal-theory MDE$_{80}$ is 0.4576 log point.  The continuous within-family companion is more diffuse and has MDE$_{80}=0.0313$ per one within-family standard deviation, but it imposes a common slope across families.

\input{tables/r3_appendix_family_information_precision.tex}

The direct-tail population retains only occupations in the four spanning families that belong to Q1 or Q5.  It contains 29 occupations and 5.03 percent of primary preperiod stock.  In the SOC2-by-calendar-month model its coefficient is 0.1494, with interval $[-0.1694,0.4682]$.  This benchmark is valuable because it exposes direct overlap; it is not a preferred estimate for the original population, and the interval includes large effects of either sign.

Table~\ref{tab:app_direct_tail_occupations} names every occupation supplying
that comparison.  The imbalance is economically visible: for example, the
health-support Q5 endpoint consists only of medical transcriptionists, while
its Q1 endpoint includes nursing assistants and home health aides.  Naming the
membership clarifies what the direct-tail estimate compares; it does not make
the sparse endpoints representative of their broad families.

\subsection{Supported edges and broader beta support}

The fully interacted within-family model yields 89 supported pairwise
quintile-edge functionals.  All 89 estimates and their standard errors are
retained in the machine-readable results.  They are distinct family-specific
comparisons rather than repeated national Q5--Q1 estimates.  Their joint
covariance has rank 50, so an 89-degree-of-freedom Wald test is invalid and is
blocked in the results rather than replaced by a selected list of pointwise
$p$-values.  The associated 50-dimensional family-specific coefficient system
is reported separately with rank-aware inference.

Table~\ref{tab:app_pairwise_aggregates} gives a transparent summary of the
supported edges.  Each row averages only family-specific contrasts for which
both endpoint quintiles are observed, using fixed preperiod endpoint-stock
weights.  Because the eligible family set changes across rows, these
aggregates do not impose a common national quintile profile and cannot be read
as ten estimates of one population parameter.

\input{tables/r3_appendix_pairwise_aggregates.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:app_broader_support} asks whether sparse tails arise solely from
requiring Webb software exposure.  Relaxing that requirement expands the
population from 468 to 490 occupations, but the added occupations represent
only 1.88 percent of broader-support preperiod stock.  Direct tails expand from
29 to 62 occupations and from 5.03 to 12.61 percent of stock because SOC43
joins the four original tail-spanning families.  The overlap therefore improves
at the margin but remains confined to five of 23 families.  Coefficients change
little in this descriptive population expansion; this is not a bound and does
not make the two populations equivalent.

\subsection{Full exposure profile}

The pooled Q2--Q5 coefficients are jointly different from zero
($\chi^2_4=11.1383$, asymptotic $p=.0251$, multiplier $p=.0212$), but pairwise
movements do not establish a monotone dose response.  After SOC2-by-calendar-
month conditioning, the four coefficients are jointly indistinguishable from
zero ($\chi^2_4=2.0708$, asymptotic $p=.7227$, multiplier $p=.7422$).  The
four-coefficient paired movement likewise does not reject
($\chi^2_4=5.4169$, asymptotic $p=.2471$, multiplier $p=.2368$), despite the
single Q5 movement's pointwise interval excluding zero.  Table~\ref{tab:app_profile}
reports pointwise and simultaneous intervals and the common-draw movement for
every group.

\input{tables/r3_appendix_profile.tex}

The continuous companion demeans beta within family using preperiod employment weights and imposes a common linear slope.  With SOC2-by-calendar-month conditioning, the rebuilt coefficient is $-0.0025$ per 0.1084 raw beta units.  Its interval is $[-0.0240,0.0191]$ in that standardized within-family unit.  The unconditioned continuous coefficient is essentially zero as well.  This is a different functional form from the tail contrast.

\subsection{Exact numerator, denominator, and family accounting}

Table~\ref{tab:app_stock_accounting} separates the model-free aggregate
Q5--Q1 stock-ratio movement into its young numerator and older denominator.
The older tail difference reinforces the negative young movement but is
smaller in absolute magnitude.  A second exact accounting separates changes
in within-family young-to-older ratios from changes in older family weights.
Neither accounting is forced to equal the nonlinear regression target, and no
component is interpreted as a causal percentage explained.

\input{tables/r3_appendix_stock_accounting.tex}

An outcome-free design audit explains why this accounting is not presented as
two saturated single-age regressions.  On the 52,884 single-age
occupation--month rows, unrestricted occupation-by-month indicators span the
entire row space and absorb all five requested exposure-by-post columns.  An
additive occupation-plus-calendar-month companion retains rank five for those
columns, but no outcome-bearing companion coefficients were estimated.  The
table therefore reports exact stock identities under the paper's normalization,
not a regression decomposition of the nonlinear grouped-binomial target.

\subsection{Leave-one-family and occupation influence}

Leave-one-family-out estimates are computed from the same family-conditioning program and reported with the omitted family's stock and target-information contribution.  These rows answer whether a broad comparison is concentrated; they are not used to choose which families belong in the main estimate.  Table~\ref{tab:app_lofo} displays the eight largest absolute movements.  Omitting office/administrative support or food preparation moves the coefficient in opposite directions by about 0.04 log point, and every paired deletion interval includes zero.

\input{tables/r3_appendix_lofo.tex}

Figure~\ref{fig:influential_between_family_paths} displays the economically
relevant endpoints behind those two largest leave-family-out movements: Q5
office/administrative-support stocks and Q1 food-preparation stocks.  These
families do not themselves span both tails, which is precisely why their paths
inform the broad between-family comparison rather than the sparse direct-tail
benchmark.  The selection is outcome-informed and the figure is diagnostic;
it neither identifies a pandemic mechanism nor supplies a deletion rule.

Occupation leave-one-out calculations are interpreted in the same way.  Fast-food and counter workers have high signed influence because low-exposure service employment recovered differently after the pandemic.  Other occupations push in the opposite direction.  Historical-contract diagnostics show that deleting the five largest absolute prior influences moves the coefficient toward zero, whereas deleting ten or twenty can make it more negative.  Symmetric influence trimming stays near the pooled estimate.  Because every such deletion is outcome adaptive, none replaces the broad-family regression.

Economically defined service exclusions are cleaner diagnostics but still change the population.  Removing Q1 food-service occupations attenuates the historical-contract absolute coefficient by less than one tenth; removing the broader SOC35/37/39 set of food, building-and-grounds, and personal-care service occupations does not attenuate it.  Protective-service occupations (SOC33) are not part of that exclusion.  These results reject a one-occupation explanation, not hypotheses about reopening, immigration, wages, or labor supply that the CPS design does not separately identify.
