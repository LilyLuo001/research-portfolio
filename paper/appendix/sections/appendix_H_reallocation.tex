\section{Reallocation agreement and the hard benchmark}\label{app:reallocation}

Among 108,500 realized occupational switches on six-way support, at least one architecture pair assigns opposite exposure directions to 53.28 percent. Pairwise official-weight Cohen kappa ranges from 0.125 to 0.932; standard unweighted Fleiss kappa is 0.501. Pair-specific support is retained rather than silently projecting every pair onto a smaller universal support.

The hard benchmark stratifies by age group, month, origin broad occupational family, and destination broad occupational family. Within 30,170 strata it preserves official-weight detailed origin and destination marginals while destroying observed detailed pairing. Hamilton expansion uses 200,000 pseudo-units and 999 fixed-seed rematches; 84,192 detailed joint cells and 98.31 percent of switch weight are represented. The benchmark mean is 52.32 percent, leaving only a 0.96 percentage-point realized excess. The persistent-switch gap is 0.88 points. Architecture disagreement is frequent, but actual pairings are not meaningfully unusually conflict-heavy under this benchmark.
