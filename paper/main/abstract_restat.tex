Occupational AI-exposure indices construct different empirical treatments from different technologies, task or ability primitives, aggregation rules, and taxonomies. Holding a national employment-stock design fixed, I find substantial divergence in occupational content and rankings. Yet six architectures produce negative high-versus-low early-career employment point estimates on literal common support. That directional agreement does not make the measures interchangeable: the same occupational movements are frequently ranked in opposite directions, and conditional family content differs in one bounded exploratory decomposition. Alternative constructions can therefore preserve an aggregate tail contrast while changing the comparisons and interpretations attached to it. Robustness is statement-specific.
