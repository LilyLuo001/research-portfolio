\section{Synthesis, Limits, and Conclusion}\label{sec:competing}

The application yields three levels of conclusion. First, construction choices demonstrably change the empirical treatment. Taxonomy routes determine which occupations enter; external concepts change tail memberships and coefficients; the Q1 reference supplies a material share of the original contrast; and comparison-age composition changes the magnitude relative to the oldest workers. These are not generic caveats. They are measured links between upstream choices and downstream claims.

Second, some narrower statements survive declared perturbations. The primary beta coefficient remains negative after calendar repair, reasonable minimum-size thresholds, stable-taxonomy and post-2020 comparisons, food-service, computer/math, office-support, and approximate leisure/hospitality exclusions. Within the selected six implementations, every common-support point estimate is negative. The task-family $D+\lambda S$ grid is also uniformly negative. Those statements must carry their architecture set, support, reference tail, age comparison, and descriptive interpretation.

Third, the stronger claims do not survive. The predeclared all-six simultaneous band fails. Outside architectures are smaller and imprecise. Q5 is not detectably different from Q2 or Q4. A within-family permutation does not demonstrate AI specificity. F/G asymmetry becomes imprecise when beta or broad is removed. Mobility conflict is frequent at zero but declines with economically meaningful thresholds and does not change aggregate moving-higher shares much. The evidence therefore does not justify a causal AI effect, a universal family ordering, a monotone dose response, or general occupational-rank interchangeability.

The contrast with prior labor-market evidence is consequently bounded. ADP payroll data can measure firm and hiring margins unavailable in CPS \citep{brynjolfsson2026canaries}. Danish adoption-linked administrative data can estimate earnings and hours effects conditional on reported use \citep{humlum2025still}. This paper shows that changing exposure construction inside one CPS stock design changes the apparent conclusion. It cannot determine which difference in data, country, outcome, adoption, or architecture explains disagreements across those studies.

Conventional uncertainty is incomplete as well. The reported intervals condition on selected exposure labels, mappings, and support; no validation sample reveals a true latent exposure. One- and two-way clustered checks do not reconstruct the full CPS survey design. Realized precision was more than three times worse than prospective calculations, and the available simulations do not identify why. These limits reduce confidence rather than supplying a preferred interpretation.

The resulting methodological lesson is simple. A constructed exposure should be reported as an architecture: technology scope, primitive, label source, aggregation, taxonomy, support, normalization, and representation. Common support is useful but does not make ranks or tail membership common. An aggregate coefficient can be stable within a selected architecture family while an external architecture, reference group, or mobility threshold changes the economic statement.

The durable conclusion is thus measurement-centered. The early-career CPS pattern is a useful stress test, not an identified AI displacement estimate. Robustness belongs to a specified statement and construction, not to the name of the variable.
