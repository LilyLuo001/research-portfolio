\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:competing}

Public CPS data reproduce a negative young-relative employment-stock association under a widely used occupational AI-exposure construction. After repairing the calendar, the top-versus-bottom beta-quintile contrast is $-0.1346$ log points. Implementing the public top-two-versus-bottom-three grouping used by \citet{brynjolfsson2026canaries} gives a smaller but negative $-0.0728$ contrast. These are descriptive population comparisons, not replications of proprietary hiring results or causal AI estimates.

The decisive fact is where the comparison lies. Broad two-digit occupation-family controls reduce the detailed Q5--Q1 coefficient to about $-0.032$. The paired movement is detectable, while the residual coefficient is imprecise and draws on only 30 percent of baseline target information. Only four broad families span both exposure tails. Thus, the baseline pattern is principally a comparison across broad occupational families; the design does not precisely isolate a detailed within-family AI gradient.

That conclusion should not be embellished into a mechanism the data reject. Low-exposure food-service occupations matter, but removing them attenuates the point estimate by less than one tenth. Broader in-person-service exclusions do not attenuate it. Joint influence exercises show large, oppositely signed occupation contributions rather than one occupation whose removal makes the result vanish. The evidence supports a broad-composition interpretation, not a demonstrated food-service-recovery explanation.

Nor do alternative exposure estimates permit a horse-race winner. All seven paired beta-minus-alternative Q5--Q1 intervals include zero, with minimum detectable differences as large as 0.169 log points. A confidence interval containing zero means that the design does not detect a difference; it does not establish economic equivalence. The original six implementations are also highly dependent, with two components accounting for 96.11 percent of their score variance. Claims of six independent validations or universal architecture robustness are therefore retired.

The measurement work remains useful precisely because it distinguishes objects that should not be pooled. Exact-code matching across incompatible occupational vintages is an implementation error. Repairing scores on fixed support is a measurement comparison. Readmitting occupations changes the population. Switching from Q5--Q1 to top-two-versus-bottom-three changes the estimand. Moving from LLM task acceleration to patent overlap changes the construct. Rotating $F/G$ into $A/E$ changes only coordinates. The revised tables report these layers rather than treating every row as interchangeable robustness evidence.

The paper's claim is consequently narrower and stronger than before. The early-career CPS pattern is real as a descriptive broad-occupation contrast, but this design cannot attribute it specifically to detailed AI exposure. Future causal work needs observed adoption or deployment, a credible source of timing or assignment variation, and outcomes that directly measure hiring and separation. Future measurement work needs occupation-task validation labels collected independently of the constructed indices, repeated ratings or audited use logs, and a taxonomy-stable holdout sample. Until then, occupational AI coefficients should travel with their construct, mapping, support, comparison, and resolution limits.
