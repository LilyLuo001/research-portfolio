\section{Data, Calendar, Taxonomy, and Samples}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{CPS employment stocks and calendar repair}

The public-data application uses IPUMS basic monthly CPS records from January 2017 through July 2026. Employed respondents ages 18--65 with positive final weights are aggregated into occupation-by-age-group-by-month stocks. The headline sample defines young workers as ages 22--25 for comparability to \citet{brynjolfsson2026canaries}; ages 18--21 enter a sensitivity. The outcome is an employment stock, not an individual employment probability. It can change through entry, exit, occupational switching, or the older comparison stock.

The original wide extract accidentally requested the March ASEC sample (`03s`) rather than the basic sample (`03b`) in 2017--2021. The ASEC observations have zero basic monthly final weights and are unusable for this design. A separately hashed repair restores the five basic March samples while preserving the frozen extract. October 2025 is categorically different: no CPS was collected during the federal government shutdown. The corrected raw files contain 9,843,021 records and 114 observed survey months. The frozen static model has 108 months because it also excludes transition month December 2022; the corrected-calendar sensitivity has 113.

Restoring the March samples changes the primary coefficient from $-0.1311$ to $-0.1346$ (95-percent interval $[-0.2230,-0.0461]$). The calendar omission does not explain the result. An attempted occupation-by-age-by-month-of-year sensitivity, adding 5,148 nuisance parameters, did not converge and is reported as a failed diagnostic.

\subsection{Taxonomy repair}

The CPS uses Census 2010 occupation codes through 2019 and Census 2018 codes thereafter, while source exposures use SOC vintages. Pre-2020 records are route-expanded using official conversion proportions, then mapped to the common target taxonomy. In one-to-many cases, the same source conversion weights apply to young and older workers. This mechanically preserves a source occupation's young/older ratio across its target components before those targets receive other sources. Fifty-six source codes are one-to-many, maximum multiplicity is seven, and they represent 20.03 percent of early-period weighted employment.

This bridge matters most through support. A literal AIOE SOC-2010 to post-2018 exact-code merge covers only 3.33 percent of computer-and-mathematical employment; the documented repair covers 97.7 percent. In a separate continuous decomposition, repairing scores on the original 410-occupation support changes the coefficient from $-0.01885$ to $-0.01920$. Expanding to 495 occupations changes it to $-0.03156$; excluding computer/math yields $-0.02940$. The main effect is re-admission, not score correction among already matched occupations. Exact-code matching across incompatible vintages is therefore documented as a failure mode, not treated as a defensible preferred estimate.

Two alternative harmonizations bound the bridge. Restricting to the Census-2018 period produces $-0.1211$ on 457 occupations and 77 months; aggregating to a stable Census-2010 taxonomy gives $-0.1522$ on 408 occupations and 113 months. The former changes the temporal estimand and the latter sacrifices detail.
