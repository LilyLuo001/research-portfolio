\section{Occupational Mobility and Ranking Statements}\label{sec:reallocation}

\subsection{Linked switches and two threshold concepts}

The CPS rotation design supplies exact adjacent-month links for valid months-in-sample; the eight-month gap from month four to five is excluded. December 2019--January 2020 is excluded symmetrically where a destination code crosses taxonomies. Origins use longitudinal weights. The all-six frame contains 108,500 employed-to-employed occupational switches; it does not contain entrants from nonemployment.

At threshold zero, an architecture assigns a switch a positive, negative, or tied direction. Six-way directional conflict---at least one positive and one negative label---is 53.28 percent by official weight. Movement-mass weighting, with mass equal to the sum of absolute standardized score movements across architectures, lowers conflict to 40.10 percent. The no-alpha set has 35.02 percent zero-threshold conflict and 15.35 percent mass-weighted conflict; within AIOE the corresponding shares are 11.35 and 2.10 percent. Changing the number and composition of possible pairs is part of those comparisons.

Figure~\ref{fig:mobilitythreshold} distinguishes two referee-requested threshold definitions. A material-move restriction first requires at least one architecture to exceed the threshold and then computes any directional conflict. Substantial opposition requires both a positive and a negative movement to exceed it. On all switches, substantial opposition falls from 53.28 percent at zero to 43.35, 29.79, and 14.00 percent at 0.1, 0.25, and 0.5 standard deviations. At 0.25 SD, 97.42 percent of switch weight is eligible and conditional substantial opposition is 30.58 percent. Percentile-rank thresholds deliver the same qualitative decline.

Immediate A--B--A reversals have 55.15 percent zero-threshold conflict; persistent A--B--B changes have 54.46 percent. At 0.5 SD, substantial-opposition shares are 13.57 and 14.39 percent. Persistence therefore does not make conflict disappear, and an immediate reversal is not classified as a coding error. Named nontrivial disagreements include accountants to bookkeeping clerks, for which AIOE falls while alpha and beta rise; registered nurses to medical and health-services managers, for which AIOE rises and alpha falls; and retail salespersons to cashiers, where AIOE falls, alpha rises, and beta/broad fall.

\subsection{Aggregate direction and the hard benchmark}

The disagreement has limited aggregate consequences in this frame. Across the six architectures, 49.21--50.49 percent of switch weight moves toward higher measured exposure, and 20.0--23.4 percent lands in the destination top weighted-rank quintile. Architecture choice changes individual labels more than the aggregate upward/downward balance. Entrant destination classifications cannot be computed from the switch-only frame.

The hard rematching benchmark preserves age-by-month, broad origin and destination families, and detailed origin/destination marginals while destroying the detailed pairing. On 30,170 strata, 200,000 pseudo-units, and 999 draws, it represents 98.31 percent of switch weight and gives expected conflict 52.32 percent (across-rematch SD .00054; Monte Carlo SE of the mean .000017). The realized 53.28 percent is 0.96 percentage points higher. This is a near-threshold sample difference, not equivalence or proof of negligibility.

The benchmark now prohibits identical detailed origin and destination codes and repairs assignments while preserving stratum margins; no false self-switches remain. The exact product-marginal expectation, 50.29 percent, permits self-transitions with probability 3.66 percent and therefore does not apply to the constrained null. Pseudo-size checks at 50,000 and 100,000 units represent 73.86 and 95.30 percent of weight and leave gaps of 0.92 and 0.91 points. The conclusion is that broad assortativity explains most excess conflict, while the exact magnitude remains benchmark-dependent.
