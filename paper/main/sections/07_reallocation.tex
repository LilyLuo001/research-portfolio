\section{Dynamics, Flows, and External Comparison}\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{Dynamics}

The binary post coefficient is not an event-study design.  I estimate quarterly coefficients for all four nonreference quintiles, omitting 2022Q4 (October--November; December is the transition month).  The Q5 path has 23 preperiod and 15 postperiod coefficients in pooled and family-month specifications.

Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics} shows the rebuilt Q5 path.  Joint tests reject that all 23 preperiod coefficients equal zero in both the pooled ($p=.0149$) and family-conditioned ($p=.0053$) models.  An insignificant anchored linear slope cannot override those unrestricted tests.  January 2023 therefore fails the parallel-evolution diagnostic for causal timing.

The calendar-weighted post functional is distinct from the nonlinear static coefficient.  It is $-0.120$ ($[-0.264,0.024]$) when pooled and $-0.207$ ($[-0.425,0.010]$) after family conditioning.  Official \texttt{HonestDiD} 0.2.8 applies smoothness and relative-magnitude restrictions to the full event vector and covariance.  Both the full 2017Q1--2022Q3 calibration and a consecutive 2021Q1--2022Q3 post-reopening calibration are reported.  Under the latter, the family-conditioned smoothness interval is $[-0.772,-0.007]$ when differential-trend bias is restricted to be exactly linear ($M=0$), but expands to $[-1.100,0.207]$ at the first positive grid value, $M=0.005$.  Both conventional intervals already include zero, so no positive zero-exclusion threshold exists.  The exercise cannot repair rejected preperiod equality or identify adoption.

Onset estimates from November 2022 through June 2023 are similar within each structure, but common timing does not distinguish AI from coincident shocks.  Ending in December 2024 gives a pooled coefficient of $-0.111$ ($[-0.201,-0.021]$); its paired movement relative to the July-2026 estimate is $0.021$ ($[-0.007,0.049]$), so the design does not detect a change from extending the endpoint beyond 2024.  Splitting the postperiod instead gives $-0.111$ in 2023--24 and $-0.168$ in 2025--26; their paired difference is $-0.057$ ($[-0.125,0.012]$), so the official-weight design does not detect a change between eras.  Family-conditioned endpoint changes remain near zero, and survey-gap exclusions are small.  A coding-stable post-2020 fit gives $-0.118$ pooled and $-0.030$ with family-month effects; their paired movements from the full-window models are $0.014$ ($[-0.029,0.057]$) and $-0.009$ ($[-0.105,0.088]$).  Quintile- and occupation-specific month-of-year controls move either coefficient by at most $0.0011$.  All six replacement fits pass the same numerical certification; the earlier failed attempts remain in the failure registry rather than being treated as results.

\subsection{Linked CPS margins}

Table~\ref{tab:flows} estimates distinct adjacent- and twelve-month margins using validated links and official longitudinal weights.  Every primary interval includes zero.  Normal-theory MDE$_{80}$ values range from 0.115 to 0.336 log point, so these are low-resolution nondetections rather than evidence of absent flow responses.

Employment exit starts from employed origins; reported occupation change requires employment at both endpoints; entry destination conditions on nonemployment-to-employment links and measures allocation across exposure groups.  The last is neither a finding probability nor employer hiring.  Twelve-month links are endpoints, not sums of monthly transitions, and retain 50.98 percent of eligible young origins versus 70.55 percent of older origins.  Hours ($-0.119$, $[-0.295,0.057]$) and conditional weekly earnings ($-0.0245$, $[-0.1024,0.0534]$) are also imprecise and condition on employment; the earnings series ends in March 2023 and contains only three postperiod months.

All flow exposure is defined at the origin where an origin occupation exists.  No current occupation is imputed to a nonemployed respondent.  Annual endpoints miss intervening jobs and transitions, and positive longitudinal-weight eligibility changes the population; neither horizon is a mechanical decomposition of the stock coefficient.

\subsection{Payroll bridge and exposure architecture}

BCC's August 2026 19-percent ``kept pace'' shortfall is a stock-growth comparison, not this regression coefficient or a hiring rate.  Complete public membership and tie rules are unavailable, so I use an equal-occupation, tie-preserving approximation and label it a bridge.  Its top-two-versus-bottom-three CPS coefficient is $-0.0720$ ($[-0.1218,-0.0222]$); family-month conditioning gives $-0.0168$ ($[-0.0810,0.0474]$), with an undetected paired movement.  Differences in universe, ages, outcome, endpoints, employer controls, and worker--firm flows remain.

That grouping bridge is distinct from changing the regression objective.  Giving each of the 468 occupations equal total weight, while retaining \texttt{WTFINL} for within-cell age shares, changes the pooled Q5-by-post coefficient from $-0.1321$ to $-0.0833$; the paired difference is $0.0488$ ($[-0.0604,0.1581]$).  The family-month coefficient changes from $-0.0217$ to $-0.0047$, with paired difference $0.0170$ ($[-0.1587,0.1927]$).  These intervals are nondetections, not evidence that the objectives are equivalent.

The bridge therefore asks whether a closer public grouping changes the CPS stock pattern; it does not calibrate CPS transitions to proprietary employer flows.  The required entry, exit, job-to-job, occupation-switching, aging, and denominator quantities are not jointly observed.

Finally, $D+\lambda S$ varies the weight on tasks requiring complementary software.  Each row uses rebuilt weights and common draws, and $\lambda=.5$ exactly reproduces beta.  Native-tail coefficients range from $-0.103$ at $\lambda=0$ to $-0.156$ at $\lambda=1$; every comparison with beta includes zero, even though 61.6 percent of stock changes quintile at $\lambda=0$.  This is large classification movement with limited coefficient resolution, not evidence about deployed technology.

Five non-$\lambda$ definitions give pooled native-support tail coefficients from $-0.099$ (direct-ability AIOE) to $-0.014$ (the reversed OECD AI-skills gap).  Every alternative-minus-beta paired occupation interval contains zero, although the OECD lower endpoint is $-0.0004$.  On the common AIOE support, some family-conditioned implementation contrasts do exclude zero; the result is therefore sensitive to how one construct is mapped in that specification, without implying that the alternatives measure the same technology.
