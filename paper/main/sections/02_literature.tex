\section{Constructs, Architecture Space, and Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Two selected families}

AIOE links progress in AI applications to O*NET abilities and combines those links with occupational ability requirements \citep{felten2018method,felten2021occupational}. The three implementations used here share that conceptual system and differ mainly in how scores are reconstructed and transported across taxonomies. Their employment-weighted level correlations range from 0.981 to 0.996. They are dependent implementations, not independent replications.

The Eloundou family begins from occupational tasks and a time-saving rubric \citep{eloundou2024gpts}. Let $D_o$ denote the share directly accelerated by an LLM and $S_o$ the share accelerated only with complementary software. Before separate standardization,
\begin{equation}
 X_{\alpha o}=D_o,\qquad X_{\beta o}=D_o+\tfrac12S_o,\qquad X_{\gamma o}=D_o+S_o.\label{eq:ds}
\end{equation}
On the 444-occupation strict common support, common mapping and aggregation preserve $X_\beta=(X_\alpha+X_\gamma)/2$ to a maximum absolute error below $7.0\times10^{-13}$. Separately standardizing the three variables breaks the numerical midpoint identity---the maximum gap is 0.5112---but creates no new primitive dimension. I therefore never enter alpha, beta, and broad together as independent primitive regressors.

Within the task family, I evaluate $X(\lambda)=D+\lambda S$ for $\lambda\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\}$ while holding mapping, support, and other design choices fixed. Fixed-beta-scale and restandardized continuous slopes are reported separately. Categorical tail estimates range from $-0.1013$ to $-0.1482$ across the grid; they need not vary mechanically with the continuous slopes because quintile membership changes.

\subsection{Outside architectures and prior computerization}

An architecture was admitted only if its construct was documented, its source was public and hashed, mapping did not rely on title matching, full components were available, quintile cuts were distinct, and preperiod employment coverage exceeded 80 percent. Webb AI patent--task overlap and the reversed OECD capability gap passed this rule. Webb software exposure remains a conditioning variable: its source correlation with Webb AI is 0.7021, substantial but far from identity. Frey--Osborne is retained as an automation-risk comparator rather than relabeled as an AI architecture. No additional LLM-labeled score was admitted after outcomes were seen.

This distinction also addresses computerization. Webb software, O*NET computer-use measures, routine-task intensity, and automation risk are not interchangeable controls. They remove different occupational comparisons. The design can ask how an AI-exposure association changes after conditioning on each; it cannot isolate a causal AI shock from a decades-long computerization process merely by adding a correlated index.

\subsection{Relation to labor-market evidence}

The most comparable descriptive benchmark is \citet{brynjolfsson2026canaries}, who use ADP payroll data through June 2026 and report a 19-percent gap for ages 22--25 in exposed occupations, operating mainly through hiring. Their revised paper examines alternative exposure measures and competing shocks and labels the evidence descriptive. The present application uses public CPS stocks, a different occupation crosswalk, and a fixed young-versus-older estimand. It does not reproduce the ADP firm or hiring margins.

\citet{humlum2025still} instead link representative adoption surveys to Danish administrative records and estimate effects on earnings and recorded hours. Their country, occupations, outcomes, observed adoption, design, and period differ from both ADP and the present application. Changing exposure construction within a U.S. CPS stock design cannot decompose the ADP--Denmark disagreement. Workplace deployment studies such as \citet{brynjolfsson2025generative} make the exposure/adoption distinction especially clear: observed tool access can affect productivity even when an occupation-level potential-exposure score is held fixed.

The broader task literature emphasizes that technologies substitute for some tasks, complement others, and induce occupational adjustment \citep{autor2003skill,autor2013growth,acemoglu2019automation}. Work on generated covariates shows why ordinary regression uncertainty generally does not absorb label-generation uncertainty \citep{battaglia2024inference,christensen2026valid,duan2026inference,ludwig2026large}. I do not observe a validated true exposure, so the paper audits architectures rather than estimating a corrected latent variable.

A targeted search of the AER, QJE, JPE, Econometrica, Review of Economic Studies, Review of Economics and Statistics, AEJ: Applied, Journal of Finance, Review of Financial Studies, and Journal of Financial Economics found adjacent work on AI deployment, automation, firm investment, remote work, and knowledge production. It did not locate a peer-reviewed article in those ten journals that fixes this CPS young-versus-older stock design while tracing exposure construction through taxonomy, reference tails, estimator information, and mobility. This is a scoped search result, not a universal priority claim.
