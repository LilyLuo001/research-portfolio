\section{Where the Employment Association Comes From}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Baseline and broad occupation families}

The substantive baseline uses the repaired 113-month calendar and frozen beta treatment definition. The Q5--Q1 coefficient is $-0.1346$ with occupation-clustered SE 0.0450 and canonical 95-percent interval $[-0.2223,-0.0468]$. The frozen design record remains $-0.1311$ with the canonical interval $[-0.2171,-0.0451]$. Calendar repair therefore does not explain the association.

The direct regression analogue of the earlier within-SOC2 permutation does. Adding broad-family-specific young-relative post terms reduces the coefficient to $-0.0315$; allowing each family its own young-relative monthly path gives $-0.0317$. Both intervals include zero. On common draws, the SOC2-by-post estimate minus baseline is $+0.1031$, with paired interval $[0.0035,0.2026]$; the monthly version gives $+0.1028$, with interval $[0.0035,0.2021]$. Thus the movement is detected even though the residual coefficient is not.

This is strong evidence about the comparison and weak evidence about a structural mechanism. In point-estimate terms, conditioning absorbs about 76 percent of the baseline magnitude. But the target changes from a cross-economy Q5--Q1 contrast to a coefficient assembled from comparisons remaining within broad families after their differential evolution is removed. Only four of 22 SOC2 families contain both Q1 and Q5, and conditional target information falls from 26.4 million to 8.0 million, about 30 percent of baseline. The conditional coefficient is connected through intermediate quintiles in most families. It should not be described as ``the remaining AI effect,'' and the difference should not be described as the causal contribution of broad composition.

The prior within-SOC2 permutation is consistent with this result but is no longer asked to carry it. Randomly reassigning detailed beta scores within broad families produces a mean coefficient of $-0.1097$ against the frozen $-0.1311$. Its exchangeability assumption is not established, so the permutation percentile is descriptive. The conditioned regressions state the relevant comparison directly and show the support and information cost.

\subsection{Profile, reference tail, and stable classifications}

The fitted post coefficients relative to Q1 are $0$, $-0.0855$, $-0.0478$, $-0.0970$, and $-0.1311$. A three-restriction test does not reject a common coefficient for Q2--Q5 ($p=.119$), but cannot establish their equality. The visible Q3--Q2 increase also does not yield a rejection of monotone decline under a least-favorable max-$t$ test ($p=.393$). The data therefore neither establish nor reject monotonicity.

Changing the requested contrast clarifies Q1's role. Q5--Q2 is $-0.0456$ with interval $[-0.1248,0.0335]$; Q5--Q4 is $-0.0340$ with interval $[-0.1110,0.0429]$; Q5--Q3 is $-0.0833$ with interval $[-0.1581,-0.0084]$. These are different economic contrasts, not alternative omitted-category parameterizations. Figure~\ref{fig:paths} shows why: both the low-exposure trajectory and the older-worker denominator matter. The fitted contrast is not simply a collapse of young employment in Q5.

Only 46 occupations are always Q1 and 18 always Q5 across the six original implementations. These stable tails account for 9.74 percent of common-support employment. Their contrast is $-0.2120$ with interval $[-0.4131,-0.0109]$, but has only 15.0 effective information occupations and a 46.7-percent top-five information share. The negative estimate survives in this narrow population; it does not show that unstable classification is innocuous in the broader one.

\subsection{Influence and the food-service hypothesis}

Influence is economically consequential but not one-directional. Jointly deleting the five occupations with the largest absolute prior leave-one-out movements reduces the estimate to $-0.1011$. Deleting the top ten or twenty instead makes it more negative, $-0.1522$ and $-0.1555$, because later occupations have opposing signed influence. A symmetric 2.5-percent trim of each signed-influence tail gives $-0.1281$, and Huber-style continuous down-weighting gives $-0.1314$. These are outcome-adaptive diagnostics rather than preferred specifications.

Cleaner occupation-side exclusions do not support the strong claim that food-service recovery explains the headline. Dropping the five Q1 food-service occupations yields $-0.1196$, an 8.8-percent reduction in absolute magnitude. Dropping all food-service occupations gives $-0.1201$. Dropping all food, building-and-grounds, personal-care, and protective-service occupations yields $-0.1370$; dropping only their Q1 members gives $-0.1387$. These exercises cannot isolate reopening, immigration, minimum wages, or labor supply, but they reject the simpler assertion that removing in-person service occupations makes the pattern disappear.
