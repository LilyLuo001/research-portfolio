\section{Employment Application: Stress Tests}\label{sec:stock}

\subsection{Baseline, architecture scope, and specificity}

The frozen primary beta-by-Webb Q5--Q1 estimate is $-0.1311$ log points (occupation-cluster SE 0.0444; wild-score interval $[-0.2170,-0.0451]$). The monotone transformation $100[\exp(\widehat\beta)-1]$ is $-12.28$ percent; transformed endpoints are $-19.50$ and $-4.41$ percent. These are relative-stock-ratio changes, not employment-probability changes, job counts, or percentage points.

On the identical 444-occupation intersection, the three AIOE point estimates are $-0.0739$, $-0.1029$, and $-0.1021$; the task-family estimates are $-0.1013$, $-0.1290$, and $-0.1465$. All are negative, but the predeclared simultaneous-band criterion fails as described in Section~\ref{sec:design}. This sign pattern is within two selected, internally dependent families.

The external-architecture audit is less supportive. Webb AI gives $-0.0649$ with interval $[-0.1439,0.0141]$ on 448 occupations. The reversed OECD capability gap gives $-0.0110$ with interval $[-0.0864,0.0645]$. On common supports, beta is more negative by 0.0646 and 0.1115, respectively; both paired intervals include zero, the OECD comparison narrowly so. The six-measure sign statement therefore does not generalize to every admissible exposure concept.

Figure~\ref{fig:benchmarks} adds generic occupational-characteristic benchmarks. On identical 363-occupation support, beta is $-0.1142$. Wage, education, cognitive content, natural telework groups, and STEM status give $-0.0185$, $+0.0093$, $+0.0270$, $-0.0240$, and $-0.0161$. Common-draw beta-minus-benchmark intervals exclude zero for education, cognition, and telework, but not wage or STEM. Telework and STEM cannot form five tie-preserving groups, so the declared natural groups replace quintiles rather than arbitrary tie-breaking.

A separate diagnostic permutes beta scores within two-digit SOC families 999 times, preserving each family's score multiset and the outcome design while reassigning detailed occupations. All fits converge. The observed coefficient is $-0.1311$ versus a permutation mean of $-0.1097$ and SD 0.0268; its lower-tail probability is .219 and centered two-sided probability .434. Because occupational score exchangeability within families is not justified, this is not randomization inference. Together, the placebos and permutation support a bounded statement: the beta contrast is more negative than several selected characteristics, but AI specificity is not established.

\subsection{Which tail supplies the contrast?}

The full beta profile relative to Q1 is $-0.0855$, $-0.0478$, $-0.0970$, and $-0.1311$ for Q2 through Q5. The Q5--Q2 estimate is $-0.0456$ with interval $[-0.1240,0.0328]$; Q5--Q4 is $-0.0340$ with interval $[-0.1141,0.0460]$; Q4--Q2 is $-0.0116$ with interval $[-0.0725,0.0494]$. Q5 differs detectably from Q3 ($-0.0833$, interval $[-0.1582,-0.0083]$), but not from Q2 or Q4. The headline therefore depends partly on Q1 rather than revealing a monotone dose response.

Figure~\ref{fig:paths} plots young stocks, older stocks, and their ratios separately for Q1 and Q5, normalized to observed 2019 and 2022 means. It confirms that the fitted contrast cannot be narrated as only a collapse in high-exposure young employment. The low-exposure reference follows its own post-pandemic path, and older stocks contribute to both ratios. These paths are descriptive and do not additively decompose a nonlinear fitted coefficient.

Occupation exclusions reinforce that reading without identifying mechanisms. Excluding food-preparation and serving occupations gives $-0.1237$; computer/math gives $-0.1158$; office/administrative support gives $-0.1163$. Excluding an approximate IND1990 leisure-and-hospitality set yields $-0.1325$ with frozen membership and $-0.1388$ after recomputing quintiles; code 800 also contains theaters and motion pictures. Deleting fast-food workers alone attenuates the original estimate to $-0.1106$. These are sensitivities, not estimates of a causal contribution.

Across the six selected measures, only 46 occupations are always Q1 and 18 always Q5. They represent 7.34 and 2.41 percent of employment. The 271 occupations reclassified between tails and middle represent 59.19 percent of employment and 82.30 percent of mean fitted influence. Common support thus fixes composition but not the treatment groups supplying the estimate.

\subsection{Age and time}

Figure~\ref{fig:agetime} makes comparison-age and era dependence explicit. The 22--25 versus 26--35 estimate, $-0.1307$, is virtually identical to the pooled 26--65 estimate; their paired difference is +0.0003. Estimates versus ages 26--45 and 36--55 are $-0.1475$ and $-0.1464$. Against ages 51--65 the estimate is smaller, $-0.0978$, and differs from the pooled contrast by +0.0333 with interval $[0.0024,0.0642]$. In the separate six-bin profile, ages 31--40 have a positive $+0.0645$ coefficient relative to 51--65. Prime-age evolution matters relative to the oldest comparison group, but the nearest 26--35 group does not explain away the pooled result.

The 18--21 versus 51--65 estimate is $-0.1050$ with interval $[-0.2251,0.0151]$. Ages 22--25 are retained for comparability and because school-to-work selection is less severe than at 18--21, not because entry occurs at one universal age.

Time heterogeneity is nonmonotone: estimates are $-0.1356$ in 2023, $-0.0783$ in 2024, and $-0.1629$ in 2025--26. The last differs from 2024 by $-0.0846$ (interval $[-0.1577,-0.0115]$), but not from 2023. The post-2025 extension therefore renews the contrast relative to 2024 rather than creating an unprecedented break. The original event study remains central: it detects no differential pretrend under its common-multiplier max statistic, but absence of a detected pretrend does not identify AI against contemporaneous occupational shocks.

For alpha, the exposure coefficient is $-0.0808$ (interval $[-0.1614,-0.0003]$) and the remote coefficient is $-0.0132$ (interval $[-0.0371,0.0108]$). The remote sign changes with architecture; neither coefficient identifies a causal channel.
