Occupational AI exposure is constructed before it enters a regression. I hold a Current Population Survey employment-stock design fixed and vary technology scope, source labels, aggregation, taxonomy mapping, support, and treatment representation. Taxonomy repair raises computer-and-mathematical employment coverage from 3.33 to 97.7 percent, mainly by readmitting occupations rather than changing scores on fixed support. Within two selected exposure families, six implementations yield negative high-versus-low point estimates on common support, but a broader architecture audit changes the conclusion: Webb AI estimates a smaller, imprecise contrast and an OECD capability measure estimates nearly zero. The original $-0.1311$ log-point beta contrast is also reference-dependent---Q5 differs from Q1 and Q3, but not detectably from Q2 or Q4---and a broad-family permutation diagnostic does not establish AI specificity. Ranking disagreement is common but scale-sensitive: six-way switch conflict is 53.28 percent at a zero threshold, 40.10 percent when weighted by movement mass, and substantial opposition falls to 14.00 percent at a 0.5-standard-deviation threshold. The evidence supports a measurement-centered conclusion: robustness belongs to a specified architecture, support, reference group, and economic statement, not to the label ``AI exposure'' in general.
