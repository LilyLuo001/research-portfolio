I rebuild a widely discussed young-worker employment pattern in public Current Population Survey data and ask whether detailed occupational AI exposure carries information beyond broad occupational composition. In the corrected January 2017--July 2026 panel, the post-January-2023 young-to-older employment-stock contrast between the highest and lowest Eloundou exposure quintiles is $-0.1346$ log points. Conditioning on two-digit occupation-family-specific post changes reduces it to $-0.0315$ (95-percent interval $[-0.1676,0.1046]$); allowing each family its own monthly young-relative path gives $-0.0317$. The reduction is statistically detectable in a paired comparison, but the conditional models retain only 30 percent of baseline target information and change the estimand. Food-service exclusions explain little of the reduction. Architecture comparisons are less informative than their point estimates suggest: the baseline design has an 80-percent minimum detectable effect of $0.1244$ log points, and paired differences across seven alternative constructions all include zero. A public implementation of Brynjolfsson--Chandar--Chen's top-two-versus-bottom-three exposure grouping yields a smaller $-0.0728$ CPS contrast. Taxonomy repair and construction choices remain consequential, but the defensible conclusion is narrower: the CPS association is principally a broad-occupation comparison, not an identified effect of detailed AI susceptibility.
