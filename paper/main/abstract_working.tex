Recent evidence associates generative-AI exposure with weaker employment among workers in their early twenties. I reconstruct the public monthly Current Population Survey comparison and ask which occupational contrasts support it. The estimand is the post-December-2022 change in the log conditional-mean employment-stock ratio of ages 22--25 to ages 26--65 in the highest relative to the lowest GPT-4-exposure quintile, conditional on intermediate quintiles and preperiod software exposure. Its estimate is $-0.132$ log points. Broad occupation-family paths reduce it to $-0.022$; the paired movement excludes zero under the occupation-clustered procedure, but direct tail support covers only 29 occupations and cannot resolve a within-family gradient. Preperiod computer-use conditioning makes the association more negative, industry conditioning moves it toward zero, and preperiod dynamics reject parallel evolution. Linked CPS estimates do not distinguish exit, reported occupation change, or entry destinations. The pattern is therefore a descriptive comparison across broad occupational structure, not an identified causal AI effect or a replication of employer hiring results.
