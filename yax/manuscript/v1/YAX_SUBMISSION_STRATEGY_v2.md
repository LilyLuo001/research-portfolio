# YAX Submission Strategy v2

## What changed after revision

Round two improves journal readiness without changing the evidence. The paper now has a general field-level question, an exact derivation of effective identifying support, separate continuous and quintile estimands, a concise nearest-neighbor literature structure, and an explicit explanation of how rank divergence can coexist with sign robustness.

This makes a *Review of Economics and Statistics* submission more defensible. It does not remove the two constraints that determine placement: the employment design is observational, and the paired beta-alpha test does not establish downstream magnitude divergence or equivalence.

## Ranked outlets

| Rank | Journal | Round-two assessment | Main fit | Main rejection risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Labour Economics* | Strong realistic first submission | Important labor application plus a reusable occupational-measurement contribution | Referee asks for worker-flow interpretation |
| 2 | *Review of Economics and Statistics* | Defensible aspirational first submission | General lesson for constructed treatments, national evidence, transparent identifying-support audit | Integrated audit judged insufficiently consequential without causal identification or paired divergence |
| 3 | *ILR Review* | Strong realistic destination | Technology, occupations, early careers, and careful interpretive limits | Measurement architecture may need more institutional translation |
| 4 | *Journal of Human Resources* | Serious but less natural stretch | Early-career employment and treatment measurement | Stock outcome cannot separate entry, exit, and switching |
| 5 | *Journal of Labor Economics* | Poor current fit | Important topic | No exogenous shock, realized adoption, or labor mechanism |
| 6 | *AEJ: Applied Economics* | High stretch | Potentially broad measurement lesson | Evidence remains observational and the principal cross-measure contrast is imprecise |

## Recommended first submission

**Labour Economics** remains the objective first-submission recommendation when placement probability and time matter. Round two improves exposition rather than the underlying identification, so it does not justify moving the modal placement upward.

If the author values option upside and can absorb one additional review cycle, submit once to **Review of Economics and Statistics**, then move directly to *Labour Economics*. The ReStat cover letter should lead with the general treatment-measurement problem and Test B, not with a causal AI-employment claim.

## Positioning sentence

> The paper shows that alternative constructions of occupational AI exposure encode different economic objects and different effective identifying comparisons, while a common national labor design produces sign robustness without magnitude or causal invariance.

## What not to promise

- A causal effect of AI on individual employment.
- A first young-worker AI result.
- A first comparison or harmonization of exposure indices.
- Economic equivalence of alpha and beta.
- A uniquely correct measure of either AI exposure or computerization.

## Submission sequence

**Placement-weighted:** *Labour Economics* → *ILR Review* → strong applied/methods field outlet.

**Upside-weighted:** ReStat once → *Labour Economics* → *ILR Review*.
