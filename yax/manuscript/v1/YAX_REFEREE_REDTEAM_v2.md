# YAX Referee Red-Team v2

This assessment reviews the round-two manuscript as submitted evidence, not as a research plan. It recommends no regression fishing.

## Overall assessment

The revision materially improves the paper. The main contribution is now legible: constructed AI-exposure treatments differ in what they encode and in which occupations supply residual identifying variation, yet the early-career employment-stock sign survives. Section 4 removes a consequential econometric ambiguity by separating the per-SD and Q5–Q1 parameters. Section 6 turns “effective occupations” from an intuitive label into a reproducible diagnostic.

The evidence has not changed. The ceiling remains constrained by observational identification and by the absence of detected beta-alpha consequence divergence. The paper's strongest defense is not that every coefficient changes, but that it shows exactly what changes in the empirical object and what does not.

## Review of Economics and Statistics

### Is the main contribution sufficiently clear?

Yes. The three-part hierarchy—different X, different identifying design, and sign robustness without magnitude invariance—is now suitable for a general applied audience. The broader lesson for constructed treatments helps the paper travel beyond the immediate AI debate.

### Is Test B genuinely new and useful?

Useful, yes; genuinely new as an integrated application, probably. The exact inverse-Herfindahl diagnostic is simple, transparent, and pre-outcome. Its value lies in pairing concentration with named occupations and showing that nominally similar models can embody different comparisons. A skeptical referee may say the formula itself is not novel; the manuscript should not claim otherwise. The contribution is making effective identifying support a central reporting object in cross-measure labor inference.

### Does computerization sensitivity strengthen or weaken credibility?

Both. It strengthens the measurement thesis because it demonstrates that the conditioning variable is part of the estimand. It weakens any singular causal-sounding interpretation because beta moves by more than a factor of two. The revision handles this correctly by refusing to select a “true” coefficient.

### Is the employment application sufficiently important?

Potentially. Early-career employment and generative AI are important, and the similar-order comparison with BCC raises relevance. Whether that is sufficient depends on whether the editor views a nationally representative observational stock design as an application or merely an illustration.

### Is causal language appropriately limited?

Yes. The draft consistently uses “gradient,” “association,” and “employment stock”; it separates entry, exit, and switching and does not claim that remote work is ruled out.

### Single most likely rejection reason

The editor or referee concludes that the paper provides an exceptionally careful measurement audit but not a sufficiently consequential new economic result because the direct paired downstream difference is inconclusive.

### Qualitative R&R assessment

**Low-to-modest but credible for an aspirational submission.** The draft is now serious enough to submit, but the modal outcome remains rejection because the contribution is integrative and the labor design noncausal.

## Journal of Human Resources

### Is the main contribution sufficiently clear?

Yes, although it is measurement-first rather than human-capital-mechanism-first. The revised opening makes the labor application subordinate to a treatment-measurement question, which is intellectually honest but may be a fit risk.

### Is Test B genuinely new and useful?

Yes for applied labor researchers using occupational indices. The named occupations are especially useful because they connect an abstract residual diagnostic to the comparisons that make the coefficient.

### Does computerization sensitivity strengthen or weaken credibility?

It strengthens transparency and weakens interpretive precision. JHR referees may appreciate the direct response to the advisor-style “is this computerization?” question, but may also ask what labor mechanism distinguishes these controls. The paper properly says it does not know.

### Is the employment application sufficiently important?

The topic is important, but the outcome is a stock rather than a worker transition. The inability to separate entry, exit, and switching is particularly costly at JHR, where human-capital and employment-flow interpretations matter.

### Is causal language appropriately limited?

Yes. The limitations are unusually disciplined.

### Single most likely rejection reason

The employment-stock estimand cannot identify whether young workers are less likely to enter, more likely to exit, or simply switching occupations, leaving the human-resources mechanism underdeveloped.

### Qualitative R&R assessment

**Low.** The paper is credible, but the journal fit is less natural than ReStat or a strong labor field journal because the central novelty is measurement architecture rather than a worker-level mechanism.

## Journal of Labor Economics

### Is the main contribution sufficiently clear?

Yes, but it is not the contribution JLE is most likely to reward. The paper asks what the treatment is and where variation comes from; JLE is likely to ask what firms or workers do in response.

### Is Test B genuinely new and useful?

Methodologically useful, but unlikely to substitute for a labor mechanism. A JLE referee can accept Test B and still regard it as diagnostic infrastructure.

### Does computerization sensitivity strengthen or weaken credibility?

It strengthens the honesty of the measurement analysis and weakens the claim that the paper has isolated a stable AI-specific labor parameter.

### Is the employment application sufficiently important?

The question is important. The design is unlikely to be sufficient because it lacks realized adoption, firm responses, worker flows, productivity, wages, or a quasi-experimental shock.

### Is causal language appropriately limited?

Yes. The restraint, however, leaves a descriptive result that may not meet JLE's mechanism threshold.

### Single most likely rejection reason

No exogenous adoption or capability shock and no worker-flow evidence connect the stock gradient to a labor-demand mechanism.

### Qualitative R&R assessment

**Very low.** JLE is not the recommended outlet on the current evidence.

## Is the paper important without detected consequence divergence?

It can be important for a field journal and potentially for ReStat because three downstream facts remain consequential: harmonization changes estimand composition, computerization architecture changes magnitude substantially, and every exposure architecture preserves the negative sign. The result is not “measurement does not matter.” It is that different measurement objects can produce the same direction while leaving meaning, support, and magnitude non-invariant.

## Best journal fit

**Labour Economics** remains the strongest fit-probability combination. *ILR Review* is close. A one-shot ReStat submission is now more defensible because the manuscript articulates a general empirical-design lesson, but it remains aspirational rather than the modal placement.

## Biggest remaining manuscript-only improvement

Make the effective-support idea effortless for a nontechnical reader. In typesetting, Figure 2 should appear immediately after the formula and before the full 30-row table. One sentence should repeatedly anchor the intuition: the count is the number of equally influential occupations that would generate the observed concentration of residual treatment variation. This is a presentation change, not a request for new analysis.
