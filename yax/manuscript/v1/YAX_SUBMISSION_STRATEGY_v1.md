# YAX submission strategy v1

Assessment based on the completed 6,351-word first manuscript and the separate referee red-team. The paper is a measurement-and-identification contribution using a labor-market application; it is not a causal mechanism paper.

## Recommendation

**Recommended first submission: *Labour Economics*.**

This is the best current balance of fit, ambition, and probability. The journal can value a nationally representative labor application, transparent measurement, and a careful observational design without requiring the mechanism or quasi-experimental leverage that a top labor outlet is likely to demand. The paper's integrated exposure-measurement contribution is stronger than a conventional robustness note, and its limits are stated in terms familiar to the journal.

If the author's objective is to maximize upside and a longer review sequence is acceptable, a single aspirational submission to the *Review of Economics and Statistics* before *Labour Economics* is defensible. It should be understood as a calculated stretch, not the modal placement.

## Journal-specific positioning

### Labour Economics

Lead with the early-career employment debate, but frame the contribution as a solution to a labor-empirical problem: studies call differently constructed indices “AI exposure,” so their coefficients may not refer to the same occupational comparison. Emphasize the complete Test A/Test B audit, the mapping composition result, all-specification sign robustness, and the remote-work comparison. Keep git governance in the appendix.

Suggested cover-letter sentence:

> We show that widely used occupational AI-exposure measures encode different occupational content and draw identification from different occupations, yet a common nationally representative design produces a robustly negative post-ChatGPT young-relative employment-stock gradient; its magnitude remains sensitive to both exposure and computerization definitions.

### ILR Review

Translate technical measurement language into implications for interpreting evidence on technology and career entry. Explain the occupations that carry identification and why stock changes can represent switching rather than unemployment. The remote-work rivalry and limits of potential-exposure indices will be especially important.

### Review of Economics and Statistics

The paper must read as a general empirical-methods lesson with a high-value application, not a dissertation robustness chapter. Figure 2 and the mapping decomposition must do most of the persuasive work. The likely desk-review question is: “What economic conclusion changes?” The answer is that estimand composition and magnitude change while sign does not; whether that is enough is uncertain.

### Journal of Human Resources

The paper needs its employment-stock interpretation on the first page. JHR readers will reasonably ask for entry, separation, and switching outcomes. Because those were not frozen and cannot be added as confirmatory evidence, the submission must sell measurement of the treatment as the central human-resources contribution.

### Journal of Labor Economics

The fit is limited without a mechanism. A JLE referee can accept every measurement result and still conclude that the paper does not explain labor demand, task reorganization, or worker transitions. Submission is not advised on the current evidence.

### AEJ: Applied Economics

The integrated chain is potentially broad, but the current result remains observational and the paired cross-measure contrast is inconclusive. The expected-value case is weaker than ReStat because the paper is not built around a quasi-experiment or a major new economic mechanism.

## Submission sequence

Two rational sequences depend on the author's time objective.

**Placement-weighted sequence:**

1. *Labour Economics*
2. *ILR Review*
3. *Economic Inquiry* or another strong applied/methods field outlet

**Upside-weighted sequence:**

1. *Review of Economics and Statistics* — one attempt
2. *Labour Economics*
3. *ILR Review*

The first sequence is recommended for a third dissertation chapter where completion and credible placement matter more than maximizing journal-option value.

## Pre-submission writing checklist

- Obtain human coauthor/advisor-style feedback on whether Figure 2 is intelligible without repository context.
- Typeset the manuscript and move detailed freeze and hash language to the reproducibility appendix.
- Keep all 30 identifying-support architectures available; use a compact main panel only if the full table remains in the appendix.
- Audit every use of “effect,” “employment,” and “equivalence.”
- Make the BCC comparison explicitly non-replicative and non-benchmarking.
- Keep the nearest-neighbor matrix available for the cover letter and response to referees.
- Revoke the credentials previously pasted into chat before any public repository or submission workflow.
