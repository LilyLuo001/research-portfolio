# CPS occupation-coding and longitudinal-link audit

The executed longitudinal design uses origins in MISH 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, or 7 and requires the same nonzero `CPSIDV` exactly one calendar month later with destination MISH equal to origin MISH plus one. It excludes the eight-month MISH 4→5 rotation gap and does not bridge the missing October 2025 sample. The December 2019→January 2020 pair is retained for employment exit, which does not consume destination occupation, but excluded from switching, occupational outflow, and realized-transition analyses because the occupation taxonomy changes.

This choice is aligned with official CPS documentation. Census describes dependent interviewing as importing the prior month's main-job industry and occupation information to determine whether a respondent has the same job and to reduce inconsistent re-entry. The interviewer's manual applies the procedure in continuing interviews 2–4 and 6–8; Census also states that it is not used in MISH 5. Thus the design benefits from dependent interviewing for eligible continuing adjacent interviews, but the first interview after the rotation gap is not treated as comparable evidence.

The official `LNKFW1MWT` is assigned at the origin after validated linkage. The realized-switch sample contains 108,500 six-way-scored switches. Immediate A→B→A reversals occur for 9.865% of eligible sequences. The separately fixed persistence sensitivity requires A→B→B when a legitimate third adjacent interview exists; it changes the main organizing contrast only slightly (75.53 versus 75.93 percentage points across the predeclared consensus-movement bins).

Preferred manuscript language: “The adjacent-month design benefits from CPS dependent interviewing and excludes the eight-month rotation gap, but occupational coding error remains possible; persistence-based results provide a sensitivity check rather than a correction for all misclassification.”

Official documentation consulted: U.S. Census Bureau, *Collecting Data* (CPS methodology); U.S. Census Bureau, *Current Population Survey Design and Methodology*, Technical Paper 66; and the Basic CPS Interviewer's Manual, section 4.E.
