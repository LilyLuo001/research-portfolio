## Assessment of the revised manuscript

The previous agent made substantial progress: the preperiod treatment was rebuilt; direct within-family support was quantified; computer-use and other characteristics were examined; alternative inference, unrestricted dynamics, HonestDiD, and linked flows were reported. This was not a cosmetic revision.

However, two revised PDFs did not establish full completion of an assignment that also required executable code, input/result lineage, run evidence, a response matrix, and a delivered replication package. Missing delivery is not proof that the files never existed. It is a limit on what could be verified.

The manuscript remained an advanced intermediate draft rather than a verified, submission-ready completion. The highest-priority unresolved issues were:

1. **Static/dynamic reconciliation.** The static pair moved approximately −0.132 to −0.022 under family-month conditioning, whereas the reported dynamic post average moved −0.120 to −0.207. The conditioned path was below the short omitted reference in much of the preperiod as well. Reference and weighting explanations required quantification.
2. **Inferential validation.** The adverse simulation showed high rejection rates for a normal interval but did not establish performance of the exact wild procedures supporting the main claims, particularly the paired movement. The household analysis used a different family-post target and only 199 refits.
3. **Incompatible conditioning modules.** The focused and expanded characteristic analyses used different group-construction conventions. The combined smaller-support characteristic/family-post estimates were not near zero, so the isolated family result was not a universal summary of conditioned specifications.
4. **Generated pandemic controls.** The shortfall regressors were estimated from related CPS outcomes, held fixed in resampling, and insufficiently investigated for shared sampling error and regression to the mean.
5. **Outcome coverage.** The earnings analysis ended in March 2023. Official EARNWEEK2 documentation indicated a concrete route to later data, subject to extract access and comparability checks. Annual link timing across onset and coding transitions needed explanation.
6. **Support and economic content.** The table showed families and quintiles present, not the full count-and-employment family-by-quintile matrix. The article still needed an account of which numerator, denominator, and between-family labor-market movements supported the pooled association.
7. **Nonconvergence.** The post-2020 benchmark was substantively important and remained missing. Preserving a failed run was appropriate but did not diagnose separation, rank failure, poor conditioning, or the solver.
8. **BCC comparison.** Family conditioning of the closer grouping was an improvement. Its paired movement remained unresolved. Feasible endpoint/population alignment and direct examination of BCC's own public evidence were still insufficient.
9. **Reporting.** Continuous AIOE units, historical service/influence results, service labels, canonical interval rounding, undisplayed sensitivity results, repeated qualifications, and poor float/table layout remained to be addressed.

The strongest candidate contribution was a descriptive account of which comparisons occupational AI-exposure regressions actually contain. A near-zero coefficient after conditioning was not evidence that occupational composition displaced AI as the explanation. The title still implied an either/or comparison that the model could not adjudicate.

## Observable failure patterns versus unknown causes

Observed patterns were topic coverage without resolution, disclosed target substitutions, historical results surviving upstream repair, stopping at a failed solver, treating the extract boundary as a data boundary, and describing rather than delivering replication evidence.

Actual internal causes—such as context truncation, haste, compute limits, poor module ownership, or stale caches—were not established from the PDFs. V3 requires investigation of those possibilities rather than assigning motives.

The previous prompt itself was broad. It did not supply sufficiently concrete cross-module acceptance tests, hard target-equality checks, or minimum evidence requirements for feasibility exceptions. The new process must repair these weaknesses instead of assuming that a longer or more emphatic instruction is enough.

## New methodological safeguards introduced in V3

These are the current assistant's methodological additions, not claims that either referee already proved a coding error:

- A simulation must define the population or pseudo-true target of each fitted model and the paired difference. A zero structural AI parameter does not guarantee all those targets are zero.
- For genuinely nested grouped-binomial designs on the same stocks, score-moment preservation supplies a powerful check: projecting dynamic fitted stocks back onto the static design should reproduce its optimum under the applicable existence conditions.
- Exact aggregate numerator/denominator accounting is distinct from coefficient decomposition; unrestricted occupation-month effects prevent a single-age exposure-post regression from supplying the requested same-model split.
- Fitted information changes can reflect both nuisance projection and changed curvature; use common-reference curvature when making a purely design-space comparison.
- Missing outcomes in linkage can create bias of unknown direction, and basic probability bounds may imply unbounded log contrasts. Do not clip them into apparently informative finite intervals.
- Every same-target comparison needs matching support, membership, scale, calendar, objective, and uncertainty definition. A disclosure of changed targets is not fulfillment of a same-target request.
