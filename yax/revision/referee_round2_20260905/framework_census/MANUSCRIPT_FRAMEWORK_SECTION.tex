\subsection{Which operation changed? A taxonomy for constructed treatments}
\label{sec:operation-taxonomy}

Comparisons among constructed treatments can change different economic
objects.  I represent the pipeline as
\[
C\xrightarrow{M}x^N\xrightarrow{H}x
 \xrightarrow{\Omega,w}x|_{\Omega}\xrightarrow{R}Z
 \xrightarrow{Q,P}\theta\xrightarrow{B}b .
\]
Here, $C$ is the technology construct; $M$ maps labels and occupational
primitives into a native score; $H$ harmonizes occupational taxonomies;
$\Omega$ and $w$ define support and its weighting; $R$ supplies a continuous,
ranked, or categorical representation to the outcome model; $Q$ and the target
population $P$ define the economic comparison $\theta$; and $B$ denotes the
coordinates used to report an otherwise fixed model.

This notation separates seven operations that should not share one robustness
interpretation.  An \emph{implementation error} is a discrepancy between the
declared and executed pipeline, such as exact-code matching across incompatible
taxonomies.  It should be corrected, not promoted to a defensible alternative.
A \emph{same-construct measurement change} keeps $C$ fixed while changing
labels, inputs, reconstruction, or aggregation in $M$.  A \emph{construct
change} instead changes the technology boundary, primitive, label meaning, or
horizon.  A \emph{support change} changes the occupations or population over
which the statistic is defined.  An \emph{estimand change} changes the outcome
or economic comparison, such as Q5--Q1 versus Q5--Q2.  A \emph{representation
change} replaces levels with ranks, quintiles, or another non-invertible feature
map.  Finally, a \emph{pure reparameterization} applies an invertible change of
coordinates to the same fitted model.

These labels are a change vector, not necessarily mutually exclusive.  A
taxonomy repair may both correct an implementation error and re-admit
occupations, thereby changing the target support.  I isolate those consequences
by first changing mapped scores on the original support and only then expanding
support.  Likewise, changing the omitted quintile is a coordinate change, but
asking for Q5--Q2 instead of Q5--Q1 changes the comparison itself.

In this application, the three AIOE implementations are maintained as
alternative measurements within one ten-application/ability construct.  The
Eloundou scores share task labels but change technology scope:
\[
X_o(\lambda)=D_o+\lambda S_o,
\]
where $D_o$ is direct LLM task acceleration and $S_o$ requires complementary
software.  Patent--task overlap and the OECD capability gap change the
primitive and technology horizon more fundamentally.  They are therefore
different constructs evaluated in a matched design, not interchangeable noisy
measurements of one observed true exposure.

\paragraph{Positive-affine invariance.}
Fix a finite occupational support, positive weights, and a deterministic
tie-preserving weighted-rank rule.  If $x'_o=a+b x_o$ with $b>0$, every pairwise
weak ordering and tie is preserved.  Weighted ranks and weighted-quintile
assignments are therefore identical.  This proposition does not cover a change
in support, weights, missing-value handling, or tie rules.  In particular,
standardization alone cannot explain changed tail membership on fixed support.

\paragraph{Piecewise-constant categorical treatment.}
For $X_o(\lambda)=D_o+\lambda S_o$ on fixed support, pairwise order can change
only at
\[
\lambda_{oj}={D_j-D_o\over S_o-S_j},\qquad S_o\ne S_j.
\]
There are finitely many such crossings.  Between adjacent crossing values the
complete ordering, weighted ranks, and rank-defined bins are constant.
Consequently, a categorical outcome specification that otherwise remains
fixed has the same objective and estimate between relevant membership
breakpoints.  This does not imply that the coefficient is monotone in
$\lambda$, and it does not apply to continuous-score regressions.

\paragraph{Coordinate invariance.}
If $\widetilde Z=ZR$ for nonsingular $R$, and sample, weights, nuisance
regressors, objective, and constraints are fixed, then $Z\beta=\widetilde
Z\widetilde\beta$ for $\widetilde\beta=R^{-1}\beta$.  The attainable indices,
fitted values, and maximized objective are identical; covariance matrices and
contrasts must be transformed with the coordinates.  Thus the family rotation
$F=(A+E)/2$, $G=(A-E)/2$ is not new outcome evidence.  When the implemented
regressors are $F/s_F$ and $G/s_G$, its exact family coordinates are
\[
\gamma_A={1\over2}\left({b_F\over s_F}+{b_G\over s_G}\right),\qquad
\gamma_E={1\over2}\left({b_F\over s_F}-{b_G\over s_G}\right).
\]

This taxonomy changes the interpretation of the audit.  Same-construct
comparisons assess operational measurement sensitivity.  Cross-construct
comparisons show how results differ when the technology question changes.
Support and representation checks show how that question is transported into
the empirical population and coefficient.  Coordinate rotations provide no
independent corroboration.  Every empirical table below identifies which of
these layers changes and which remain fixed.

\subsection{A bounded architecture census}
\label{sec:architecture-census}

The implemented set contains six selected scores from two dependent families,
not six independent validations.  On their exact 444-occupation support, two
weighted principal components account for 96.11 percent of score variance.
Two external constructs---Webb AI patent--task overlap and the reversed OECD
capability gap---passed the previously declared source, mapping, completeness,
coverage, representation, and matched-design screen.  Frey--Osborne fails that
screen's technology-scope condition because it is a broad automation-risk
measure, and remains a comparator rather than an AI architecture.

This is not a census of the global literature.  The repository's dated source
audit also identifies evidence-grounded retrieval, reinforcement-learning
feasibility, startup-targeted, platform-usage, query-based, and other
constructions for which YAX has not completed a hashed source-file, mapping,
support, and admission audit.  Those candidates are pending rather than failed.
The revision reports the candidate ledger and separates source integrity,
construct classification, mapping/support feasibility, and representation
feasibility.  Passing those operational checks establishes comparability to the
declared YAX workflow; it does not establish that the candidate measures the
same latent technology or is closer to a true exposure.
