Date: 2026-09-05

This matrix covers every numbered **major comment** in the two full reports supplied on 2026-09-05. `RR1-M#` denotes the report recommending reject-and-resubmit with twelve major comments; `RR2-M#` denotes the report recommending rejection in its present form with nine major comments. The reports' secondary and presentation comments are intentionally outside this major-comment matrix.

All new outcome analyses in this round are post-outcome exploratory. The frozen design, confirmatory results, and first-round revision outputs remain immutable comparison artifacts. Existing results are inputs, not evidence that the new requests have already been satisfied.

## Priority and status conventions

- **P0 — framing gate:** must be completed before choosing the revised paper's center of gravity or making comparative claims.
- **P1 — essential response:** needed for a credible major revision, but it does not determine the framing by itself.
- **P2 — conditional or scoped response:** valuable only after the P0 results, dependent on a new dataset, or best handled by demotion and precise disclosure.
- **Empirical** means a new fit, simulation, or data construction; **analytical** means a derivation or deterministic calculation; **editorial** means a change in exposition or package organization. Many rows require more than one type.
- **Ready** means the existing SCC microdata and code appear sufficient; **partial** means some requested components already exist but the report requires new work; **conditional** means an external input or a defensible design choice must first be obtained; **infeasible as stated** means the literal request cannot be supported by the available data and must not be approximated silently.

## Cross-report conflicts and principled limits

1. **Substantive composition paper versus measurement paper.** RR1's preferred paper says the CPS pattern is largely broad occupational composition plus a service-recovery reference group. RR2's preferred route is a more general framework for distinguishing construction operations. P0.1--P0.4 decide whether the first statement is empirically sustainable. The revision should not call the permutation mean a decomposition or choose either narrative before those tests.

2. **SOC2 adjustment saturation.** RR1 requests SOC2-by-age-by-post effects; RR2 proposes broad-family-specific young-relative monthly effects and acknowledges that this changes the conditioning estimand. Report both nested models if the latter retains support. A failure of the saturated model is a support result, not evidence for the baseline.

3. **IUT treatment.** RR1 invites dropping or sharply reframing the IUT; RR2 asks to preserve the mathematical distinction between the IUT and the failed predeclared simultaneous band. The compatible resolution is to retain the IUT only as a dependent-set, marginal sign statement and preserve the failed simultaneous criterion as the design record. It cannot evidence six independent confirmations.

4. **Adoption data.** RR1-M9 requests a new adoption series; RR2 says it is not a prerequisite for a measurement-centered redevelopment. Treat it as P2 scope expansion, not a condition for completing the decisive P0 analyses.

5. **Unavailable counterfactual weights.** “With and without the January 2025 control revision” is only literal if alternate weight vintages or official bridge factors exist. Endpoint and unweighted comparisons are informative but are not the same counterfactual.

6. **Unavailable proprietary replication objects.** CPS can reproduce a public BCC score/grouping rule, not ADP firm/hiring outcomes or unpublished title mappings. The response must name the reproduced component and the inaccessible remainder.

7. **Misclassification is not measured by reversals.** Immediate occupation reversals can motivate a sensitivity analysis, but they are not a validated estimate of reporting error. No attenuation correction should be presented as data-identified from that rate.

8. **Inference coverage.** Occupation/time procedures can address declared dependence models. They do not automatically incorporate CPS complex-survey sampling, exposure-label generation, or age-specific crosswalk-allocation uncertainty. Those layers must remain separately named.
