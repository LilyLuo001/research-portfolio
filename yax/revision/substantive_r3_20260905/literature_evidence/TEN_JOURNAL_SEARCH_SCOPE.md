# Declared ten-journal novelty search

**Search date:** 2026-09-05
**Status:** completed as a targeted publisher-site search; **not an exhaustive systematic review and not proof of absence**.

## Scope fixed before interpreting the search

The declared journal set is:

1. *American Economic Review* (AER)
2. *Quarterly Journal of Economics* (QJE)
3. *Journal of Political Economy* (JPE)
4. *Econometrica*
5. *Review of Economic Studies* (ReStud)
6. *Review of Economics and Statistics* (ReStat)
7. *Journal of the European Economic Association* (JEEA)
8. *Journal of Finance* (JF)
9. *Journal of Financial Economics* (JFE)
10. *Review of Financial Studies* (RFS)

This corrects the earlier repository ledger that substituted *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* for JEEA. The old ledger is not a valid record of the declared scope.

## Search protocol

Publisher-site/domain searches combined variants of:

- `"artificial intelligence"`, `"generative AI"`, and `"AI exposure"`;
- `occupation*`, `employment`, `labor market`, `young worker*`, `early career`, and `CPS`;
- `measure sensitivity`, `crosswalk`, `task exposure`, and `computerization`.

Searches included journal-domain restrictions and title/abstract review of returned results. Working papers and papers in other journals were recorded separately because they are essential intellectual neighbors even though they do not satisfy the declared ten-journal criterion.

## Directly relevant work outside the declared ten-journal test

The novelty assessment must also acknowledge:

- Brynjolfsson, Chandar, and Chen (August 2026), [*Canaries in the Coal Mine?*](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/), including monthly CPS and ACS comparisons;
- Acemoglu, Autor, Hazell, and Restrepo (2022), [“Artificial Intelligence and Jobs: Evidence from Online Vacancies”](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/718327), *Journal of Labor Economics*;
- Webb (2020), Eloundou et al. (2024), and Felten, Raj, and Seamans (2021), which supply the exposure architectures;
- the CPS analyses cited by BCC itself in Online Appendix §H.

These papers make any generic claim such as “the first public-data study of AI exposure and young workers” false or, at minimum, indefensible.

## Narrow claim supported by this search

The most that the dated search record supports is:

> In targeted publisher-site searches through September 5, 2026, we did not locate a peer-reviewed article in the declared ten journals that combines a national monthly CPS young-versus-older employment-stock design with systematic within-design variation across named occupational exposure architectures and mapping/support constructions.

Required qualification:

> This targeted search is not exhaustive and does not establish priority for the broader young-worker or public-CPS pattern.

## Preferred manuscript practice

Avoid “first,” “novel,” and “no prior paper” formulations unless they are tied to the narrow, dated protocol above. The safer contribution sentence is affirmative rather than priority-based:

> We hold a public-CPS employment-stock design fixed and quantify how its result changes across named exposure architectures, mapping/support rules, and occupational conditioning restrictions.

That statement describes what YAX does and remains true if another related paper is later located.
