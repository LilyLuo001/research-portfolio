# YAX Phase 3 hard-benchmark decision

**POST-OUTCOME EXPLORATORY — NOT PART OF CONFIRMATORY YAX v1.1.**

## Decision: HB-C

The realized six-architecture directional-conflict rate is 53.28%. The earlier benchmark, which independently rematched origins and destinations while preserving their marginals within age-by-month cells, has a mean of 45.27%, yielding the previously reported 8.02 percentage-point gap.

The predeclared harder benchmark additionally preserves origin broad occupational family and destination broad occupational family. Across 999 fixed-seed constrained-rematching draws, its mean conflict rate is 52.32% and its 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles are 52.21% and 52.43%. The realized-minus-hard-benchmark gap is therefore 0.96 percentage points. This is below the predeclared 1.00 percentage-point meaningful-gap threshold, so the binding classification is **HB-C**.

The persistent-switch sensitivity gives the same decision. The realized conflict rate is 54.46%, the hard-benchmark mean is 53.58%, and the gap is 0.88 percentage points.

The descriptive empirical upper-tail probability is 0.001 in each sample. This does not overturn HB-C. The constrained-rematching distribution is extremely precise because it uses 200,000 pseudo-units and 999 draws; the tail area answers whether the realized rate differs from this particular computational reference distribution, not whether the remaining 0.96-point gap is economically meaningful. It is neither a conventional sampling p-value nor causal evidence.

The primary benchmark represents 98.31% of official switch weight, spans 30,170 strata and 84,192 detailed joint cells, and leaves zero false self-switches after the frozen repair rule. The persistent benchmark represents 99.92% of official weight. The predeclared fallback was not used.

## Manuscript consequence

V5 must not claim that realized occupational pairing is unusually conflict-heavy in a meaningful magnitude after conditioning on broad origin-destination assortativity. It may retain the established descriptive fact that alternative exposure architectures frequently classify the same realized switch in opposite directions and that this fact survives pair-specific support. It may also report that most of the original 8.02-point excess over independent rematching is accounted for by broad occupational assortativity.

No further benchmark is authorized.
