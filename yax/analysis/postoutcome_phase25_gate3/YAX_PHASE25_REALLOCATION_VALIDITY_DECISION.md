# YAX Phase 2.5 — Reallocation Validity Decision

**POST-OUTCOME EXPLORATORY — NOT PART OF CONFIRMATORY YAX v1.1**

## Decision: REALLOC-R1 — STRONG

The three predeclared classifications are:

- **SUPPORT-S2:** selection is visible but bounded and interpretable;
- **PAIR-P1:** low-agreement/high-conflict patterns survive on broad pair-specific support;
- **BENCH-B1:** realized transitions are meaningfully more conflict-heavy than the matched benchmark.

Pair-specific support retains 59.54%–90.80% of weighted harmonized switches, versus
58.25% on the literal six-way intersection. Across all 15 frozen pairs, changing from
six-way to pair-specific support moves weighted agreement by at most 1.95 percentage
points. The key disagreements remain: ability-direct versus alpha agreement is
56.32%; administrative/equal versus alpha is 59.25%; and OEWS/source-weighted versus
alpha is 59.53%. Internal AIOE agreement remains much higher (88.85%–96.02%), while
beta–gamma agreement is 87.07%.

The common-support sample is occupation-family selected, so the claim must name its
support. But it is close to excluded transitions on age, period, young × post,
persistence, and reversal status. Pair-specific expansion does not erase the
architecture disagreement, and realized pairings exceed the matched-marginal conflict
benchmark by 8.02 points (8.46 points for persistent switches). Together these facts
satisfy `REALLOC-R1`.

The admissible substantive statement is narrow: among harmonized realized occupation
switches scored by the relevant frozen architectures, exposure definitions often
disagree on whether a move raises or lowers measured exposure, and actual transition
pairing is more conflict-heavy than a fixed marginally matched benchmark. This is not
a treatment-effect estimate and not a causal result.

Because `REALLOC-R1` is returned, a future proposal may examine destination cost using
one predetermined measure such as pre-period OEWS wages. That extension was not
executed here.
