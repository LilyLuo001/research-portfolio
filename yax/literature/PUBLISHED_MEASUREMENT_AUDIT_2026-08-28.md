## Binding implications

1. YAX must never describe the 3.33% naive exact-code AIOE merge as a mistake
   made by BCC, EIG or Budget Lab. Each uses an explicit repair or
   reconstruction.
2. The legitimate question is whether **alternative defensible mappings and
   common-support rules** materially alter X, its identifying occupations, and
   the downstream coefficient.
3. BCC's 19% headline is not the numerical SESOI benchmark: it is a top-two
   versus bottom-three descriptive shortfall and is not on YAX's
   young-relative-to-older Q5--Q1 estimand.
4. EIG, Budget Lab, Pulito and BCC mean that "same outcome under multiple
   measures" is not novel. YAX must report direct paired coefficient
   differences on fixed support and connect them to Tests A and B.
