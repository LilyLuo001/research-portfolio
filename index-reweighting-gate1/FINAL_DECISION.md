# Gate 1 decision memo: rule-based index reweighting assignment and support

Execution date: 2026-09-08  
Primary screening window: 2018–2025  
Separate partial extension: 2026 through the last verified calendar material in the package

**Final recommendation: REVISE / RESOLVE A SPECIFIC INPUT GAP**

## Decision

The institutional variation is sufficiently concrete to justify one more bounded assignment-retrieval exercise, but the present evidence does not support an outcome-measurement pilot. The audit verifies relevant local holdings and daily-market coverage, documents rule regimes and candidate events from primary institutional sources, and finds repeated calendar overlap between confirmed binding interventions and scheduled FOMC statements. It does not recover a single complete grade-A assignment vector, exact point-in-time target-index membership, or a validated counterfactual comparison. Those are prerequisites for defining treatment and determining whether any residual design variation survives mechanical reweighting, common-news exposure differences, repeated issuers, and common event dates.

This is an input-and-design gate failure, not evidence that rule-induced assignments or the proposed economic mechanism do not exist. The immediate investment should be limited to historical assignment files and the corresponding same-date rule replay. No headline return regression, treatment-effect estimate, power calculation, or intraday data acquisition is justified yet. Novelty and publication potential were not assessed by this execution audit.

The controlling numerical record is [`logs/gate1_summary.json`](logs/gate1_summary.json). Source-level claims trace to [`source_and_rule_registry.csv`](source_and_rule_registry.csv); local archive claims trace to [`local_data_catalog.csv`](local_data_catalog.csv), [`fund_identifier_and_coverage_audit.csv`](fund_identifier_and_coverage_audit.csv), and [`logs/stage_a_evidence.json`](logs/stage_a_evidence.json). Counts below describe screened rows, event groups, or meeting intersections exactly as labeled. They are not independent observations and are not a power calculation.

## What was actually established

### Audited local-data facts

The documented SCC archive and manifest were physically accessible. The audit read the archive in place, wrote only to the separate run directory, exported no raw archive rows, and found no new files written to the archive during the final run. The baseline manifest contained 12,100 rows and the post-snapshot `near_taq` area contained 195 files. The canonical MIDAS selection contained 168 monthly files; two superseded quarterly copies were identified and not stacked. Three overlapping candidate file pairs were checked by full-file SHA-256 and were byte-identical, so they were also not stacked.

The candidate-fund screen covers QQQ and eleven Select Sector SPDR funds for eight calendar years, yielding 96 fund-year audit rows. It located 59,605 holdings rows across 1,133 fund-report dates in 41 Parquet parts from 24 completed batches; each batch's recorded part and row totals reconciled to Parquet metadata. There were no duplicates on the audit's documented fallback holdings key. Of those holdings rows, 57,072 mapped to PERMNO, and each mapped row found a nonmissing CRSP price on the latest trading day no more than seven calendar days before the report date. The lowest fund-year row mapping and price-match share was 85.44%; the lowest absolute-percent-TNA mapping and price-match share was 95.27%.

These are useful coverage facts, not assignment evidence. Thirteen fund-year cells each lacked one eligible calendar month. More broadly, 94 of 96 cells had at least one month, identifier-map, or source-coverage flag; only two were complete for the recorded screening fields. Every one of the 96 cells is separately marked `NOT_EXACT_INDEX_ASSIGNMENT_INPUT`. The holdings schema contains report and effective dates but not verified filing, first-publication, or download-vintage timestamps, so a report date cannot be described as the trader information date.

Candidate ETF daily coverage comprises 24,016 security-days. Returns are complete except for one XLC fund-year cell with 134 of 135 returns; recorded open and bid/ask pairs are complete in the audited fund-year cells. This verifies daily fields only. The MIDAS files are daily summaries linked by ticker, not modern intraday quotes, trades, order imbalance, or synchronized cross-market paths.

The targeted membership search found one Nasdaq-100 name match in local index metadata, but zero constituent rows for that metadata key, and found no Select Sector name match. Generic Compustat or CRSP index-membership rows were not relabeled as the target families. This statement is deliberately scoped to the manifest, schemas, and candidate tables inspected; it does not claim that an authoritative constituent history does not exist elsewhere. Effective-date CRSP/Compustat links are available for reconciliation, but they are not assumed to reproduce an index provider's company aggregation or float treatment.

### Audited rule and event facts

The rule registry contains 21 source/regime rows: ten with grade-A rule-source support, seven with grade-B support, and four unresolved. These grades describe the evidence for a rule statement, not the observability of an assignment. The registry separates company-level from security-line treatment and does not backcast the current Nasdaq-100 methodology into earlier periods. In particular, contemporaneous SEC-filed descriptions support security-level treatment in 2019 and the first quarter of 2020, an unresolved transition in the second quarter of 2020, and issuer-level treatment by July 2020. Earlier unsupported routine Nasdaq announcement dates remain blank rather than being inferred.

The event ledger has 785 index-event rows grouped into 104 event-date groups. It classifies 30 rows in 17 groups as confirmed binding, 175 rows as confirmed nonbinding, 575 as uncertain, four as outside the applicable regime, and one as a confirmed constituent-replacement intervention rather than a candidate cap treatment. The row-to-group distinction matters: multiple sector indexes subject to one common date or policy change do not create separate shocks merely because they occupy separate rows.

The most informative public pilots remain incomplete:

- The S&P August 2024 capping analysis supplies selected, rounded Technology-sector examples for March and June 2024. The dose table records 30 selected security rows plus two explicit incomplete-vector placeholders. These rows can show within-table directional cap distortions—for example, a large rounded June distortion for Apple—but the top-15 excerpts are retrospective illustrations, do not contain every constituent, and are not complete contemporaneous assignment files. They are never renormalized. The source is recorded as `SPDJI_S2_20240808` in the registry.
- The September 2024 Select Sector consultation result establishes a methodology change, the September 13 pro-forma visibility date, September 20 implementation close, and September 23 effective open. The ledger treats this as one common policy transition, not eleven experiments. It does not supply the complete old-rule and new-rule vectors on identical inputs. The source is the [official S&P result notice](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20240903-1474128/1474128_sp-select-sector-indices-results-20240903.pdf).
- The Nasdaq notice distinguishes the July 3, 2023 reference date, July 14 pro-forma release, July 21 implementation close, and July 24 effective open for the special rebalance. The dose evidence establishes the event and approximately 12% reported one-way turnover, not constituent-level exact weights. The [official special-rebalance schedule](https://indexes.nasdaqomx.com/docs/NDX_SpecialRebalance_2023.pdf) therefore does not substitute for the historical pro-forma contents.
- The July 17, 2023 Trade Desk-for-Activision replacement is separately recorded as another intervention and linked as interference with the July 24 special rebalance. It is not attributed to the special rebalance and is not counted as a cap treatment. Its provenance is the [Nasdaq replacement announcement](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/the-trade-desk-inc.-to-join-the-nasdaq-100-index-beginning-july-17th-2023-2023-07-12).

Across all pilots, [`assignment_doses_and_evidence.csv`](assignment_doses_and_evidence.csv) contains 34 grade-B rows and zero complete vectors. There are no grade-A assignment events. Consequently, the audit cannot yet calculate a complete signed rebalance vector, distinguish that vector from cap distortion for every constituent, reconcile weights at a common valuation state, or report exact issuer HHI, complete one-way turnover, assignment-boundary support, or residualized dose information.

### Audited common-news support facts

The FOMC calendar contains 78 rows: 72 in the 2018–2025 primary scope, one boundary row, and five in the partial 2026 extension. Sixty-three primary-scope meetings are eligible scheduled statement meetings. Calendar entries preserve differences among statement-only, press-conference, SEP, strategy-statement, press-release, and cancelled-meeting records, while phases of a meeting remain one common-news package. The sources point to [Federal Reserve calendars and historical materials](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), with event metadata screened against the recorded USMPD workbook registry.

The calendar join yields 11,745 support rows because it records event, meeting, and stage-anchor combinations, including unresolved events and context rows. It is not a sample-size statement. Restricting attention to confirmed binding events and primary-scope meetings, the summary reports 37, 70, and 86 distinct event-group/meeting pairs within 20, 40, and 60 trading days; those bands involve 24, 36, and 39 distinct meetings, respectively. This is positive evidence that temporal overlap is not trivially empty.

It is not yet usable causal support. Only 74 rows across the full support table have valid documented stage dates for the anticipation calculation; 8,572 have missing or invalid stage dates for that calculation, and 3,099 lack a resolved anchor. Windows can overlap other reweightings, later interventions, or common policy dates. The support table therefore preserves uncertainty rather than coding unknown anticipation as zero. It also reports zero reusable comparison identifiers. Repeated use of the same meeting, issuer, basket, or transition cannot be treated as new independent macro information.

## Evidence classification and consequences

**Proxy indications.** ETF holdings offer historical-composition screening and a check on candidate securities. The rounded S&P tables indicate that some 2024 cap adjustments could be economically nontrivial. The confirmed-event/FOMC band counts indicate that a reconstructed intervention sample could intersect a meaningful set of common-news dates. None of these objects supplies exact treatment, exogeneity, comparison validity, or power.

**Maintained assumptions.** A future design would have to assume, and then defend, that provider-rule assignments can be separated from endogenous size and return histories; that an explicit comparison captures different common-news loadings and signal precision; that shared stocks and flows do not invalidate the intended contrast; and that trader-time anticipation can be represented from contemporaneous publication records. It would also need a residual portfolio defined before outcomes, with fixed membership and an aggregation rule independent of post-treatment quote availability. Excluding the focal stock would remove its arithmetic contribution but would not automatically equalize the remaining baskets' exposures.

**Unexecuted tasks.** No complete rule replay, treatment/exposure design matrix, rank or collinearity test, leave-one-event or leave-one-issuer diagnostic, residualized information-share diagnostic, return outcome model, simulation, minimum detectable effect, intraday latency analysis, or futures-inclusive measurement audit was run. This abstention is substantive: those exercises would manufacture precision if performed before exact assignment and a frozen comparison are available.

**Direct exclusions, unresolved evidence, and current-specification failures.** The 175 confirmed-nonbinding rows and four outside-regime rows are direct exclusions from treatment; they are not failed estimates. The 575 uncertain rows are unresolved classification/input gaps, not evidence that the economic mechanism fails. The actual failures of the current specification are structural: event rows cannot be treated as independent assignments because the 30 confirmed-binding rows collapse to 17 groups, with shared sector dates and one common September transition; holdings cannot stand in for rule-assigned portfolios; and daily fields cannot stand in for intraday paths. Most importantly, every one of the 785 comparison-audit rows lacks a validated `comparison_id`; 575 are blocked by unknown assignment and 30 by incomplete assignment vectors. Thus the current version has no estimable preferred causal contrast even if the calendar join is accepted. That specification could be repaired only if the next retrieval produces exact inputs and a comparison that survives the mechanical benchmark.

## Smallest next retrieval and adjudication test

The next request should be a bounded, authorized historical S&P assignment package, not a general vendor subscription. It should cover the Information Technology Select Sector Index for the March 18 and June 24, 2024 effective portfolios and the September 23, 2024 rule transition, plus any other Select Sector portfolio strictly required for the predeclared comparison. For each episode it must include every constituent; stable security, share-class, and company identifiers; point-in-time membership; unrounded pre- and post-assignment index shares or weights; the uncapped reference portfolio or the complete contemporaneous inputs needed to reproduce it; provider shares, investable-weight/float factors, prices and valuation timestamps; corporate actions between reference and implementation; the exact methodology version and operation order; and the historical pro-forma publication date and time. A later download must preserve the original file's vintage or archival provenance. ETF holdings are only a reconciliation check.

The adjudication test is mechanical and should be run before inspecting any outcome. First, put old and new portfolios on one verified valuation state and reconcile each full vector to provider totals without filling, interpolating, or renormalizing missing names. Second, replay the applicable company aggregation, caps, ranks, redistributions, and secondary checks; for September, apply old and new rules to identical inputs. Third, produce signed rebalance changes, cap distortions, issuer HHI, and one-way turnover, with precision bounds that do not flip binding status, rank order, or dose sign. Fourth, demonstrate that the reconstruction path works for at least one complete grade-A vector and is repeatable on a second routine episode rather than only the common methodology transition.

In parallel—but still before outcomes—freeze one comparison. It must name the treatment object, counterfactual portfolio, assignment and inference units, mechanical identical-price-path benchmark, controls for pre-event common-news exposure, anticipation interval, shared-stock interference rule, and strongest falsification. It must generate a nonempty `comparison_id` and a full-rank treatment/exposure matrix after the proposed fixed effects, with support reported by event group and issuer and under omission of the largest episode, dominant issuer, Technology sector, and September transition. The FOMC overlap should then be recomputed using verified trader-time publication stages.

This exact test changes the decision only if it establishes both observable treatment and a credible, repeated contrast. If the source is merely unavailable, the result remains an access gap. If complete files reveal negligible or nonrepeating signed variation, if reasonable precision changes treatment signs, or if the treatment is absorbed by event/issuer structure, that would become direct evidence against this intervention design. The separate July 14, 2023 Nasdaq pro-forma can be sought later as an institutional cross-check; one special episode should not substitute for repeated Select Sector support. No purchase, trial, vendor contact, or intraday-data order is authorized by this memo.

## Questions 2 and 3

Question 2 remains logically downstream. Daily returns could later inform exposure and covariance inputs, and simulation could expose a statistic that mistakes reweighting, heterogeneous loadings, stale quotes, or clock error for transmission. The current audit neither calibrated nor validated any intraday statistic, and it did not create pseudo-ticks from daily data. Passing an eventual simulation would be a necessary diagnostic, not identification.

For Question 3, the archive supports daily stock and ETF price, return, open, and quoted-spread screening, subject to recorded schema and identifier caveats. It does not support modern event-time stock/ETF quote and trade paths, cross-market clocks, or empirically calibrated event-level variance. It also does not establish the futures histories required for a futures-inclusive claim. A later stock–ETF-only pilot could narrow the scientific claim and avoid a futures dependency, but it would still require a frozen security universe and timestamped quote/trade fields. Measurement acquisition should wait until the assignment and comparison test above clears.

## Four closing conclusions

| Dimension | Gate 1 conclusion |
|---|---|
| Observable treatment | Not established. Thirty event rows in 17 groups are confirmed binding, but all 34 dose records are grade B, every vector is incomplete, and exact target-family membership is unresolved locally. |
| Comparison credibility | Not established. No reusable comparison identifier or estimable treatment/exposure matrix exists; mechanical weights, heterogeneous common-news exposure, signal precision, anticipation, shared issuers, and spillovers remain open. |
| Independent support | Promising but unproven. Confirmed binding groups intersect 24, 36, and 39 distinct primary-scope FOMC meetings in the 20-, 40-, and 60-trading-day screens, but meetings and shared intervention groups are dependent and many stage dates remain unresolved. |
| Future outcome measurability | Not established with the current archive. Daily screening data exist, but the required intraday paths, synchronized clocks, and event-level measurement variance do not; this question should not be funded before treatment and comparison clear. |

The earliest evidence that should trigger reconsideration is therefore not an outcome coefficient. It is a complete, source-vintaged 2024 assignment vector that reconciles under the correct contemporaneous rule, followed by the same successful procedure on a second routine episode and a frozen comparison whose identifying variation survives the mechanical and dependence audits. Until those conditions are met, the project remains an assignment-retrieval and design-validation exercise.
